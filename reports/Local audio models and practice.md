# Generate beds with ACE-Step, script everything else

Of all the open audio models on Hugging Face in November 2026, **only one music generator passes the owner's rules and runs on the M3 Max: ACE-Step 1.5** (MIT weights, nothing to credit). The first real run on 16 Nov makes it practical but not free. **Six 20 s instrumental clips took 216.9 s, 29.8 s per clip at the median, with a peak of 10.4 GB**, and swap grew to 9.8 GB because Docker was running. Every stronger open model is ruled out by its licence: Stable Audio 3 asks for a "Powered by" line, MiniMax Music 3 requires a name display and AI disclosure, and YuE2 is non-commercial. So ACE-Step is the best *allowed* model, not the best open one, and the owner's listening verdicts, still pending, are the real test. The situation is clearer outside music. **No video-to-audio (foley) model is licence-clean for an EU user**, and that costs little, because MSP already knows every animation event to the frame. **Sound effects should come from a curated CC0 kit**, with MOSS-SoundEffect v2 (Apache-2.0) only to fill gaps. The utility layer is fully covered by permissive or locally usable tools: Demucs-MLX for separation, Audiobox Aesthetics plus Qwen3-Omni as automatic judges, S-KEY and beat_this for key and beats, CLAP for picking tracks, and ffmpeg/libebur128 for loudness. The design that follows: **ACE-Step fills a pre-approved bed library in quiet-hour batches. The per-post pipeline never generates anything; it only selects, fits, mixes and masters, which is light CPU work.** The research on short video supports a lyric-free, moderately upbeat bed, an audio hit in the first fraction of a second, a fixed short sonic signature at the end, and a fixed small set of SFX, with beds rotating so loyal viewers don't tire of them.

## Only ACE-Step survives the no-credit, commercial-weights filter

The owner's rule (16 Nov) is that nothing in a post, caption or profile carries a credit. MSP is open source and not run for revenue, but it promotes a product, so non-commercial weights stay out too. A sweep of Hugging Face (text-to-audio by downloads, trending and newest; music, MLX and ACE-Step searches) found **no new open generator that passes this filter and beats ACE-Step 1.5**. The lists are dominated by the same few families and their repacks. Two findings change details, not the verdict. **MiniMax Music 3 now runs on Apple Silicon** through community MLX ports and merged mlx-audio support, but its licence still requires displaying "MiniMax-Music3" and disclosing machine-generated content ([HF mlx-community/MiniMax-Music3-8bit](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/MiniMax-Music3-8bit); [LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-Music3/blob/main/LICENSE)). A ComfyUI repack of it carries an Apache-2.0 tag, but a repack tag does not override the upstream licence ([HF Comfy-Org/MiniMax-Music-3](https://huggingface.co/Comfy-Org/MiniMax-Music-3)). **HeartMuLa** has a new Apache-2.0 Swift-MLX port that renders at 3× real time on an M5 Max. It writes songs with vocals, though: its own card says style tags "steer it weakly", that most voices come out female whatever the tags ask, and that it refuses edits. That makes it a poor fit for 8–30 s instrumental beds ([HF mlx-community/HeartMuLa-oss-3B-bf16](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/HeartMuLa-oss-3B-bf16)).

The quality context is sobering. The main human-preference board, **Artificial Analysis AA-Music-Instrumental** (launched 7 Oct 2026), is led by closed models: Suno v6 at 1148 and Lyria/Mureka around 1080. The best open entries are MiniMax Music 3.0 (1000, the anchor) and Stable Audio 3 Medium (996), both excluded here. **ACE-Step is not on the board at all** ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/music/leaderboard/instrumental)). In Stability's own paper, ACE-Step 1.5 xl-turbo trails SA3 medium on instrumentals (FAD 0.193 vs 0.107) ([arXiv 2605.17991](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17991v1)). In a small 8-system academic arena it placed 6th ([arXiv 2605.01790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01790)). The fair conclusion: ACE-Step's quality relative to the excluded models is known only from rivals' papers and small arenas, so **the MU1 blind listening, with library tracks mixed in, is the only evidence that settles whether it is "good enough as a quiet bed"**. Magenta RT 2 is the one possible second generator. The attribution duty of its CC-BY weights most likely attaches to redistributing the weights, not to posted music, but that reading is an inference and needs the owner's sign-off before any output goes out.

Community LoRAs add little. Roughly 30 genre LoRAs for XL-turbo carry no licence tag and were trained on undisclosed "synthetic tracks". Others are MIT-tagged but trained on third-party music, even if limited to "max 2 per artist" ([HF ryanontheinside ambient](https://huggingface.co/ryanontheinside/ambient-acestep1.5-xl-v1); [HF LouisAmas ambient-downtempo](https://huggingface.co/LouisAmas/acestep-1.5-xl-sft-lora-ambient-downtempo)). Only LoRAs trained on the author's own recordings are defensible, such as `ambient_dream1` (MIT, "10 short instrumental songs made and recorded by myself") ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/DisturbingTheField/ACE-Step-v1.5-ambient_dream1-LoRA)). No lo-fi or "cinematic tension" LoRA exists by name. The clean long-term route is a LoRA trained on MSP's own motif, once LoRA training works on MPS.

## The first M3 Max run: 30 s per clip, 10 GB, and the planner LM is the slow part

The first measurement (16 Nov 2026, M3 Max 36 GB, **Docker running**) used **ACE-Step 1.5 turbo (2B DiT) with the 1.7B planner LM via MLX, shift 3.0, for 20 s instrumental clips**. Six clips took **216.9 s wall in total**. Loading the models cost 33 s once (LM 12.8 s, DiT 20.2 s). Clips took **21.8–47.6 s each, median 29.8 s**, so generation ran about 1.5× slower than real time. **Peak process memory was 10.4 GB, but swap grew from 1.5 to 9.8 GB**: with Docker on, the machine was under memory pressure, and the per-clip spread (more than 2× between fastest and slowest) probably reflects that pressure as much as the model. Larger runs need Docker stopped. **Raw loudness ranged from −12.7 to −17.7 LUFS integrated, with peaks up to −0.4 dBFS.** That 5 dB spread between clips, with peaks almost at full scale before any AAC encode, means two-pass loudness normalisation is a mandatory stage, not an option. The owner's listening verdicts are still pending, so nothing is yet known about quality.

The most useful comparison comes from a Swift-MLX port of the same weights (bit-parity tested against upstream) measured on an M5 Max. There, the 8-step DiT solve for 30 s took **0.8 s** and the VAE decode about 1 s. A full 30 s render took 15.8 s, of which **13.3 s was the planner LM**: "the planner is ~80 % of a song". Requests without the planner (cover, repaint, extend) finished in **3–5 s per 30 s** ([xocialize/mlx-ace-step-swift MEASUREMENTS.md](https://github.com/xocialize/mlx-ace-step-swift/blob/main/MEASUREMENTS.md); [HF mlx-community/ACE-Step-1.5-turbo-bf16](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/ACE-Step-1.5-turbo-bf16)). A separate MLX port measured turbo 4-bit plus the 1.7B LM at about 22 s per 30 s on a base M5 ([HF roman220220/ACE-Step1.5-sft-MLX-8bit](https://huggingface.co/roman220220/ACE-Step1.5-sft-MLX-8bit)). The M3 Max run, at roughly 45 s of compute per 30 s of audio, fits that picture: an older GPU, the Python/MPS DiT path rather than Swift, and swap pressure. **The obvious next experiment is the same briefs with the planner switched off** (`use_lm=False`) and caption, BPM and key given explicitly. If the M5 ratio holds even loosely, that should bring a 20 s bed under about 10 s. That is an estimate to test, not a result. The planner may matter for musical structure, which is why the A/B has to be judged by ear and not only by time.

Several low-risk quality options can be A/B'd within the existing install. ACE-Step's official instrumental advice is turbo, 8 steps, `shift=3.0`, the deterministic ODE sampler and `instrumental=True`. It says 30–180 s "works well", which hints that very short requests are outside the sweet spot, so generating 30 s and trimming is safer than asking for 15 s ([INFERENCE.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/INFERENCE.md)). The turbo variants trade off differently. `turbo-shift3` is "clearer, richer timbre, but may sound 'dry'", and `turbo-shift1` has "richer details, but semantics weaker" ([Tutorial.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)). **ScragVAE** (MIT) is in ACE-Step's official alternative-VAE registry. It restores the high frequencies the stock decoder attenuates above about 6 kHz, which matters for crispness on phone speakers, although its gains are self-reported spectral metrics, not listening tests ([HF ScragVAE](https://huggingface.co/scragnog/Ace-Step-1.5-ScragVAE); [ALT_VAE.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/ALT_VAE.md)). DCW is an opt-in, training-free sampler correction ([DCW.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/DCW.md)).

Two known defects shape the pipeline. A documented **harmonic hum around 654–669 Hz** (−12 to −15 dB) affects XL-turbo with the Oobleck VAE. The published fix is non-commercial, so a narrow EQ notch in the mastering chain is the free substitute ([HF ACEStep-XL-Regrind-V1](https://huggingface.co/mdmachine/ACEStep-XL-Regrind-V1)). And **repaint currently re-normalises the whole clip**, which causes a loudness jump at the edit (open issue #1374), so "regenerate just the ending" must be loudness-matched afterwards or avoided ([GitHub issues](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/issues?q=instrumental+OR+ending+OR+fade+OR+hum+OR+artifact)). No open model offers a guaranteed seamless loop. The research method that does (LoopGen) runs on MAGNeT, whose weights are non-commercial ([arXiv 2504.04466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04466)). Loops are therefore built in post: generate at a fixed BPM, cut on bar boundaries, crossfade a few beats.

## Foley models are closed off; a curated CC0 kit does the job better

The idea of having a model "watch the MP4 and score it" fails on licence before it fails on Mac support. **MMAudio, ThinkSound/PrismAudio, AudioX, ControlFoley and Sony's Woosh have non-commercial weights**, and **HunyuanVideo-Foley's licence explicitly "DOES NOT APPLY IN THE EUROPEAN UNION"**, which rules it out for a Czech owner ([HF MMAudio](https://huggingface.co/hkchengrex/MMAudio); [GitHub ThinkSound](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/ThinkSound); [ControlFoley](https://github.com/xiaomi-research/controlfoley); [Woosh](https://github.com/SonyResearch/Woosh); [HunyuanVideo-Foley LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/spaces/tencent/HunyuanVideo-Foley/blob/main/LICENSE)). An independent licence audit merged on 31 Oct 2026 reached the same verdicts ([kajisho5/sound-design-skill PR #5](https://github.com/kajisho5/sound-design-skill/pull/5)). The Mac story is no better: an MPS port of MMAudio was closed unmerged in February 2026, and HunyuanVideo-Foley is CUDA-only ([MMAudio PR #97](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio/pull/97)). The loss is small. These models are trained on real-world footage, and their strength is syncing to visible physical impacts. A count-up or a rank card sliding in has no real-world sound source. MSP's renderer knows every GSAP timestamp to the frame, so placing SFX from the timeline is more accurate than any model's guess. No source has tested a foley model on motion graphics, so that last point is inference, but the licence wall makes it moot.

For text-to-SFX, two models are licence-clean. **MOSS-SoundEffect v2.0** (Apache-2.0, May 2026, 1.3B, 48 kHz, up to 30 s per call, fine-tunable, with ComfyUI nodes) is the stronger. **EzAudio** (MIT code and weights, 24 kHz) is the older fallback ([HF MOSS-SoundEffect-v2.0](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-SoundEffect-v2.0); [HF EzAudio](https://huggingface.co/OpenSound/EzAudio)). Every classic alternative (AudioLDM, Tango 2, TangoFlux, AudioGen, Auffusion, Stable Audio Open) is non-commercial or Stability-licensed ([TangoFlux](https://github.com/declare-lab/TangoFlux)). MOSS v2's reference code targets CUDA with `torch.compile`, so on the Mac it would run in eager mode on MPS or CPU, speed unmeasured ([README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/moss_soundeffect_v2/README.md)). One user reported poor prompt adherence ([issue #97](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/issues/97)). No source evaluates any of these on UI-style sounds: clicks, ticks, pops, chimes.

That settles the approach. A brand kit needs **10–20 consistent one-shots** (piece snap, tick, count-up roll, drop or thud, whoosh, chime, pop), where crisp transients and sameness matter more than variety. Picking human-recorded Freesound CC0 sounds and trimming and EQ'ing each one once is faster and more predictable than curating diffusion output. MOSS v2 earns a place only for puzzle-specific textures a library lacks, such as cardboard pieces clicking, a box lid, or pieces pouring. For those, generate 1–2 s and trim, as a quiet-hours job with the owner's go-ahead.

## Every utility step has a licence-clean tool, but judging needs two kinds of judge

The analysis and QA layer is where local models help most, and the licences mostly cooperate. Two licence readings apply throughout (inferences, not legal advice). Permissive licences (MIT, Apache, BSD) attach notice duties to redistributing code or weights, never to published audio. CC BY 4.0 weights used locally publish nothing of the weights, **but this repository is public, so CC BY weights must never be committed or vendored into it**.

Separation is now nearly free on Apple Silicon. The MLX Demucs port claims **114× real time warm on an M4 Max, 2.7× faster than PyTorch-MPS**, so separating an 8–30 s bed takes well under a second after the model loads. Its README warns that MLX can hold 16–31 GB of cache in some runs, so the cache needs a cap on a 36 GB machine ([demucs-mlx](https://github.com/ssmall256/demucs-mlx)). For ACE-Step's instrumental output, separation is mainly a **deterministic vocal detector** (vocal-stem energy against the mix) and a way to tame a busy drum part by ducking that stem 10–15 dB rather than muting it.

Judging is the subtle part. A 2026 probe of five open audio-language judges on 2,748 rated clips found that **without the audio, no judge beat a prompt-only baseline; only two judges actually followed the audio when rating musical quality; smaller judges in pairwise mode tracked which slot a clip was in, not its quality; and rewording the instruction moved correlations about as much as switching judges** ([arXiv 2610.13247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13247)). Per a secondary summary, the two judges that did follow the audio were Audio Flamingo 3, which is non-commercial, and **Qwen3-Omni**, which is Apache-2.0 ([alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.13247)). So an LLM judge earns a place only for fixed-wording, single-clip, yes/no questions, never for precise scores or A-vs-B choices. The numeric ranking across N ACE-Step candidates belongs to Audiobox Aesthetics, and glitches belong to plain DSP checks (clipping, silence gaps, DC offset, spectral-flatness spikes), because no open detector for music-generation artifacts exists. Qwen3-Omni at 4-bit is roughly 17–18 GB of weights, an estimate from its parameter count. It cannot sit in memory next to ACE-Step on a 36 GB Mac with other apps running, so it runs as its own batch step. Every threshold must be calibrated against the owner's Works / Unsure / No-go labels before it gates anything.

For mastering, no 2026 open neural model is both permissive and production-ready. A deterministic chain fits 8–30 s beds on phone speakers better anyway: high-pass, a small cut in 200–400 Hz mud, gentle presence, light compression, then two-pass loudnorm. Instagram publishes no loudness spec. Guides converge on −14 LUFS integrated and −1 dBTP or lower, and note that Instagram's ~128 kbps AAC re-encode overshoots inter-sample peaks, which is why the final measurement must be taken on the encoded file ([audobox](https://audobox.com/blog/reels-loudness-why-14-lufs-is-a-gate-not-a-creative-choice.php); [cutscore](https://cutscore.io/blog/loudness-for-instagram-reels); [Clyper](https://www.clyper.io/guides/how-to-make-a-video-louder)). The raw ACE-Step peaks at −0.4 dBFS make that point concretely.

## Research favours an unobtrusive upbeat bed, a first-second hit and a fixed signature

Most viewers hear Reels, but not all, and Stories less. Meta reported that **over 75% of Reels views are sound-on** (Nov 2024), and a Meta/Toluna study put it at **over 80%** ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-and-toluna-study-finds-emojis-lift-reels-purchase-intent-2-5x/)). For Stories the only first-party figure is old, about **60% sound-on around 2019** ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/digital/should-brands-use-captions-and-subtitles-in-video-content-for-stories/)). So one in four or five Reel views, and perhaps two in five Story views, are silent. MSP's rule that every number is readable in the frame stays right, and audio is a reward layer on top. The same Meta/Toluna study (ads, not organic posts) found that **an audio-plus-visual hook in the first 1–2 s raised the odds of top-20% purchase intent 1.5×**, and that delivering the message through both channels gave 1.8× ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-and-toluna-study-finds-emojis-lift-reels-purchase-intent-2-5x/)). Instagram lists muted Reels among the content it shows less, and its ranking runs on watch time, likes and sends ([Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)).

On what kind of music, the best experiment is narrow but relevant. In a 2024 *JCMC* study with about 620 participants, **fast instrumental music (120–160 bpm) made expert explainer TikToks more persuasive than slow music or none, with no effect on memory** ([JCMC](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/29/5/zmae007/7729237)). Multimedia-learning research is mixed. Background music can act as a "seductive detail" that hurts comprehension of demanding content ([PMC5671572](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5671572/)), yet a 2023 meta-analysis found a small positive effect overall ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369490840_The_impact_of_background_music_on_learners_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis)). A 2026 preprint on 14,492 short videos found **behavioural engagement follows an inverted U in stimulation intensity**: moderate beats maximal ([arXiv 2604.19995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19995)). For data Reels, which are simple and number-heavy, this points to a lyric-free, moderately upbeat bed (roughly 110–125 bpm) that never competes with the text, and against stacking loud music, dense SFX and fast cuts all at once. Cutting on the beat is good craft, but the "40% higher completion" figure often quoted has no traceable source ([OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-ai-beat-sync)). Snapping reveals to downbeats is worth doing because it is free in MSP's pipeline, not because it is proven to lift reach.

Sonic branding gives the strongest case for a fixed audio signature, with a large caveat about scale. Ipsos's analysis of 2,015 ads found **sonic brand cues 8.53× more likely to appear in high-performing ads, yet used in only about 6% of them** ([Ipsos PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2024-01/Ipsos_Views_Power_of_You.pdf)). Industry research favours motifs of about 2–3 s ([TADADAM](https://tadadam.com/en/blog/brand-recognition-by-sound-alone)). A peer-reviewed placement study found a happy-sounding logo works best at the end ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359553015_Small_sounds_big_impact_sonic_logos_and_their_effect_on_consumer_attitudes_emotions_brands_and_advertising_placement)). A review in *Psychology & Marketing* frames sonic identity as the sum of every sound touchpoint, not a single jingle ([Spence & Keller 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mar.21995)). Repetition research adds the counterweight: **liking rises with early exposures and then falls** ([Peretz et al.](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03201171.pdf)), and jingles are the most irritating kind of ad music ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303685286_Stop_this_music_What_are_the_antecedents_and_impacts_of_irritation_towards_advertising_music_and_how_to_prevent_it)). At about 2,700 followers MSP won't see TV-scale recognition effects. The realistic benefit is that regulars learn "the MSP sound". The practical rule is to keep the short signature and SFX identical and rotate the beds. That matters most for the weekly Story, which a loyal follower hears 52 times a year.

On AI music, audiences cannot hear the difference, but labels cost trust. In Deezer/Ipsos's 9,000-person survey, **97% failed a blind test, yet 80% want fully AI music labelled** ([Deezer newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2025/11/deezer-ipsos-survey-ai-music/)). Several advertising studies find that disclosed or perceived AI lowers authenticity and trust ([J. Interactive Advertising](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15252019.2025.2554149)). Listeners told a track is human rate it as more emotionally effective, though their measured responses don't differ ([arXiv 2506.02856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02856)). A background instrumental bed under data visuals is the lowest-risk use, which is consistent with the owner's no-disclosure decision. The residual risk is a hobby community that values human effort finding out later. Hedges help: a human-made or human-edited signature, human curation of every bed, and an honest answer if anyone asks. No study has measured that "discovered later" scenario.

## The pipeline: generate offline, assemble per post, and keep the kit fixed

Put together, the evidence implies a two-speed system. The **slow loop** is a quiet-hours batch job with the owner's go-ahead, Docker off and run metrics logged. ACE-Step generates candidate beds per mood at 30 s; Audiobox Aesthetics, the DSP checks, Demucs vocal detection and Qwen3-Omni's yes/no questions filter them; S-KEY and beat_this tag key and BPM; and the survivors go to a lab gallery for the owner's Works / Unsure / No-go. Approved beds join `library.json` next to free library tracks (Meta Sound Collection, Pixabay without the Content ID shield, Mixkit), with model, version, prompt, seed and licence recorded per bed as claim evidence. The **fast loop** runs on every post and is light CPU work, never a heavy job: CLAP ranks library beds against the draft's one-line brief after hard filters (BPM window, not used recently); the owner hears the top three; the bed is fitted to whole bars; GSAP reveals snap to downbeats; SFX are placed at timeline events; and the mix is mastered and measured on the encoded MP4. Because generation never happens at posting time, the heavy-job rule and the 24-hour fresh-data rule never collide.

The brand sound kit that follows from the evidence has four fixed parts and one rotating part. The **signature** is a 1–2 s motif of 3–6 notes, or a piece snap plus a chime, played identically at the end of every Reel and every weekly Story, in time with the logo on screen. It should be human-made or human-edited, both for distinctiveness and as the authenticity hedge. The **SFX palette** is about 10–20 CC0 or MSP-recorded one-shots (snap, tick, count-up roll, thud, whoosh about 0.2 s before cuts, chime, pop), EQ'd once, with energy in the 200 Hz–5 kHz range phones reproduce, and mono-safe. The **mood map** covers the formats. Count-ups get a steady 110–125 bpm pulse, ticks that speed up with the counter, and a hit when the number lands. Rankings build tension, drop to near-silence for 0.3–1 s before #1, then hit plus signature. Records and celebrations go brighter and major. Explainers get a sparse low-energy bed. The **rules** are: an audio transient at 0–0.3 s with the first motion, never a fade-in; the bed dipped under SFX at reveals (start at about 6 dB and tune by ear); Reel music that resolves back into its opening bar so the auto-loop feels seamless; and a Story that ends on a clean resolved button before the frame ends. The **beds** rotate: three or four per format in the same style, the weekly Story bed changed monthly or seasonally, and a fresh set for campaigns such as Advent. These mood and mix rules are craft judgement built on the research above, not findings from controlled tests.

## Conclusion

The research shifts where the value lies. Generation, the headline capability, is the most constrained part: one allowed model, unknown quality relative to the field, about 30 s per clip on this Mac, and loudness that is all over the place. Everything around it is unconstrained: fitting, snapping, mixing, mastering, measuring, separating, and even automatic screening with licence-clean judges. An imperfect generator is therefore acceptable. A screened, human-approved library absorbs its misses, and the per-post pipeline can be fully automatic without ever running a model heavier than CLAP. If the owner's verdicts on the first clips come back mostly "No-go", the same pipeline runs unchanged on free library tracks, and nothing downstream needs rebuilding.

Three open items will decide the next step. The first is the owner's listening verdicts, especially blind against library tracks. The second is a planner-off rerun with Docker stopped, which tests whether per-bed time falls from about 30 s to a few seconds. The third is a decision on Magenta RT 2's CC-BY weights as a second, motif-driven generator. For the sonic signature, the evidence suggests making it the one piece of audio no model writes.
