# Score every post from its cue sheet

**The evidence and the craft agree on a clear order: for a short motion-graphics video, the music has to (1) carry the right mood for the story beat and (2) put its accents exactly on the visual accents. Matching the tempo to how fast things move matters much less.** MSP is well placed for both. Every builder already writes a `cues.json` that marks the frames that matter (`key`, `reveal`, `snap`, `fail`, `outro`). A composer would call that file a "spotting list". In practice, Claude writes a two-part brief for each Reel and Story. A script computes the structural half from the cues: the tempo (BPM) that puts the key moments on the first beat of a bar, the bar plan, the length and the silences. Claude writes the expressive half from the storyboard: aura, scoring stance, three instruments, the ACE-Step caption and section tags. ACE-Step 1.5 XL only respects tempo roughly (±2 BPM) and gives no control over where sections fall. So the fitter (`lab/music/fit.py`), not the generator, has to make the hits land. That means one new step: detect the real tempo and stretch the bed by a few percent. The three worked briefs below compute to **104 BPM for the pile-of-shame Reel, 121 BPM for Unlucky 13, and 124 or 99 BPM for the weekly Story**. They are ready to run once the owner gives the go. Ranking the 3–4 candidates automatically before the owner listens is cheap and licence-clean with beat_this (MIT), LAION CLAP (CC0/Apache) and the fitter's own measurements. One owner decision is open: whether Audiobox Aesthetics (CC-BY-4.0) may be used as an internal tie-breaker, with a notice in the repo only and never a credit in a post. The recommendation is yes. One gap matters more than the rest: **no study links tailored music to Reels watch time or sends.** Only MSP's own insights can show that.

## Accent sync and mood fit carry the evidence; tempo matching barely registers

Film-music research separates two kinds of match, and they act through different routes. **Mood match changes how viewers interpret and remember a scene.** Boltz played the same ambiguous film clips with positive or negative music. Positive music made a man following a woman read as a long-lost lover, negative music as a threat. The effect still showed in memory **a week later**, and the soundtrack even changed which objects people remembered ([Boltz 2001, full text](https://scholarship.haverford.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?httpsredir=1&article=1019&context=psychology_facpubs)). The influence grows the more ambiguous the picture is ([Lipscomb & Tolchinsky](http://www.lipscomb.umn.edu/docs/FilmMusic_LipscombTolchinsky_final_asPublished.pdf)). That matters for MSP's text-led posts. A confession like "I will solve it this weekend" can read as funny or as sad, and for viewers with sound on, the bed decides which.

**Timing match ("accent sync") is what makes music feel like it belongs to the picture.** In rating experiments, landing musical accents on visual accents improved perceived fit more than matching musical tempo to on-screen speed, and the two effects did not interact ([Sugano & Iwamiya, CiNii abstract](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001206567584128)). Lipscomb built 56 audio-visual pairings. Aligned accents rated highest for both sync and effectiveness, and the two ratings correlated at **r = .96**. Alignment also mattered more for simple stimuli than for complex film footage ([Lipscomb pre-print](http://www.lipscomb.umn.edu/docs/ArticleSelectedReportsPrePublicationWithFigures.pdf); [JASA abstract](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/105/2_Supplement/1274/550157/Cross-modal-integration-Synchronization-of)). MSP's flat, few-object motion graphics are exactly the "simple stimuli" case. The owner's MU1 note, "the music has to hit the video", recorded on almost every clip, is that finding heard by ear. Patterns of change should match too: rising pitch or loudness fits text that grows or comes closer ([Kim & Iwamiya](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249978365_Formal_Congruency_between_Telop_Patterns_and_Sound_Effects)).

Advertising research adds that **fit stacks**. In a study of 178 viewers, each extra point of fit (music matching the narration, the product and the audience) raised attitude to the ad (η² = .12) and memory (η² = .06), a small-to-medium effect ([Herget, Breves & Schramm 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1029864920904095)). Instrument choice that suits the product improved recall and liking. Fast music reduced recall of ad content compared with slow music ([Oakes & North 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227875000_The_impact_of_background_musical_tempo_and_timbre_congruity_upon_ad_content_recall_and_affective_response)). So text-heavy slides want a moderate tempo, not "energy". The colour-to-music link runs through emotion. Lively major-key music goes with light, saturated, warm colours, and slow minor music with dark blues and greys ([Palmer et al. 2013, PNAS](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Music%E2%80%93color-associations-are-mediated-by-emotion-Palmer-Schloss/3a760f45753c3d037d8e95ef052f0749f5065eb8)). The frame's palette can therefore set the bed's mode and brightness by default.

Humour is the exception that proves the rule. **A deliberate mismatch works only when viewers can "get" it.** Incongruent music that viewers could not make sense of confused them and lowered product ratings. Mismatches that fit a familiar frame, such as action-film tropes on a coffee ad, worked ([Spence & Di Stefano 2026 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12926423/)). In a study of musical endings, unexpected but still in-key final notes were rated funnier than very dissonant ones ([BMC Psychology 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9526306/)). A thesis on TikTok-style clips found that more congruent versions were rated *less* funny ([Kent State ETD](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=kent1627070680501086&disposition=inline)). The rule for MSP: in data reveals and launches, keep the music matched. In jokes, make the mismatch recognisable within a second (mock-solemn, sneaky cartoon) and keep it in key.

Two honest limits apply. Almost all of this is lab work from 1988–2013 with film clips, not vertical phone video. And **no peer-reviewed study ties music–video fit to Reels watch time, shares or saves.** Humans judge fit reliably while models still struggle. On the HarmonySet benchmark, people scored about **85 %** on rhythm and emotion fit questions, against about **42–44 %** for Gemini 1.5 Pro ([HarmonySet, CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01725v1)). That is why the owner's ear stays the final gate and the automatic ranking only orders what he hears. The one MSP data point is the week-46 Story, the first with our own fitted bed. Its exits (21.6 %) and taps forward (55 %) sat between two Stories with app music: no warning sign, but n = 1 (`content/reports/story46-check.md`).

## Composers decide what the music does before they pick a genre

Professional scoring starts with **spotting**. Composer and director watch the cut and agree on a cue list: where music starts and stops, which frames it "hits", where its emotional job changes, and where it stays silent. "Silence is a scoring decision", and an agreed out point (where a cue ends) prevents more rewrites than an agreed in point ([Modwheel](https://modwheel.net/guides/scoring-to-picture-spotting)). Composers then choose the tempo by trying candidate BPMs against the hit points and keeping the one where most hits fall on beats. This is the classic "click" method from Karlin & Wright's *On the Track*, and calculators still do it ([Scribd excerpt](https://www.scribd.com/document/246248419/On-the-Track); [Frans Absil tempo calculator](https://www.fransabsil.nl/htm/eventhit.htm)). Ad briefs add three portable habits. Describe the music's **function** ("what is the temp track doing for this scene?") rather than a genre. Give references with a "because". And always state **what the music must not be**, which "is often clearer than what it should be" ([Studio ZK brief template](https://studiozk.nl/blog/ad-music-briefing-template); [Atomica](https://atomicamusic.com/the-best-music-brief-template-for-ad-agencies)). Short spots share one shape: no real intro, a build, a stop or drop, then a short **button** (a final sting) on the brand moment ([ScreenWeaver](https://www.screenweaver.ai/blog/writing-30-second-commercial-av-format); [That Pitch](https://thatpitch.com/blog/structuring-songs-for-easy-editing-2/)). For MSP, the button is the falling signature.

A few terms in plain words. A **beat** is one foot-tap. A **bar** is a group of four taps in 4/4 time. The **downbeat** is the first, strongest tap of a bar. **BPM** is taps per minute, so at 120 BPM one bar lasts 2 s. A **drop** is the moment the full arrangement comes in. A **stop-down** is the music cutting to real silence for effect.

Composers also choose between three stances. **Underscoring** sets a mood and moves in long phrases. **Mickey-mousing** hits every on-screen action and "is usually comedic, whether this was intentional or not" ([Wikipedia: Mickey Mousing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mousing)). Chion's **anempathetic** music goes on "like a machine that's running", indifferent to the drama. Music boxes, celestas and player pianos are classic sources, and the indifference *intensifies* the emotion rather than flattening it ([Wikipedia: Anempathetic sound, quoting Chion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anempathetic_sound)). Comedy scoring is mostly a timing problem. "Scoring under the setup announces that something's coming." A punch accent must sit "within a couple of frames" of the gag. And "a beat of true silence rather than a quiet sustain reads as a much stronger event than a diminuendo" ([Composer Agents Academy](https://agents.composerworkflow.com/academy/scoring-comedy-is-a-timing-problem/)). Danny Elfman describes the "play it straight" school: "let the comedy just be itself. Don't try to help it" ([Ain't It Cool News interview](http://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/55508)). For data explainers, Kurzgesagt gets a soundtrack composed per episode, paced to the visuals, with "deep booms on key words" ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/what-we-do?visit=videos); [10 Studio](https://10.studio/the-incredible-amount-of-work-behind-kurzgesagts-beautiful-animated-videos/)).

For MSP this becomes a division of labour. **SFX hit many things, the music hits few.** The music lands on `key` (the number is the drop), the punchline (usually as silence) and `outro` (the button). Excuses, ticks, bonks and count-ups go to the SFX layer that `lab/sfx/plan.py` already places on the contact frame. That is also all ACE-Step can do, since one drop can be aligned but fine hits can't. Comic posts follow a five-step recipe. An innocent setup that gives nothing away. Escalation hit by SFX while the bed plods on, indifferent. A stop-down 0.3–0.6 s before the punchline. The punchline in silence plus one stinger. Re-entry on the next downbeat, then the signature. The recipe is assembled from the craft sources above and is untested on MSP. **Leitmotifs**, short figures tied to an object (Wagner's spear, the ring), recur and change instrument to show change ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/leitmotif)). ACE-Step can't place a figure at a given second, so MSP's motifs (the spider, the last piece) belong in the SFX layer, built like the signature: in the bed's detected key and in the bed's instrument family.

The visual aura picks the instruments. A teaching resource on music for media gives standard associations. Bassoon is "comic, awkward". Pizzicato (plucked strings) is "light, comic, secretive". Celesta, glockenspiel and marimba give "magical, childlike, uncanny or delicate" colour. Low brass suggests menace. A filtered synth pulse suggests "time pressure". Sparse piano suggests memory ([PastPaperHero](https://www.pastpaperhero.com/resources/aqa-a-level-music-7272-music-for-media-sonority-and-timbre)). The table turns this into a lookup for the brief writer. The instrument pairings are craft. The tempo ranges are judgement and should be checked by ear in the labs, since nobody has confirmed how ACE-Step renders "music box", "harpsichord" or "bassoon".

| Visual aura | Instruments and texture words | Tempo feel |
|---|---|---|
| Cozy table, warm lamp | felt piano, warm soft plucks, brushed drums, upright bass, tape texture | 80–92, relaxed |
| Dusty, old, neglected | felt or detuned upright piano, music box, vinyl crackle, dry pizzicato | 88–104 |
| Museum, solemn (for a joke: mock-grand) | soft organ or choir pad, harpsichord, slow strings, big hall | slow |
| Sneaky, mischievous | pizzicato, bassoon, clarinet, woodblock, light marimba | 96–112, short notes |
| Time pressure | filtered synth pulse, clock tick, muted hi-hat, low drone | 110–130 |
| Neon, competitive, data | plucked synth arpeggio, tight kick, rim, glassy bells | 116–126 |
| Advent, winter, magic | celesta, glockenspiel, sleigh bells, warm strings | 90–110 |
| Halloween, eerie-funny | harpsichord, bassoon, pizzicato, theremin-like lead, celesta | 100–116, playful minor |

The **house touch** (woodblock click, soft pluck, felt piano), the signature and the reactive SFX stay the same in every post. Only the aura row changes. This follows the sonic-branding principle "fixed motif, flexible arrangement" from the earlier research.

## A script plans the bars, Claude writes the feel, the fitter makes it land

Research on generating music from video has two branches. End-to-end models (VidMuse, MuVi, Video2Music and others) need training, and their weights are mostly non-commercial: anything built on MusicGen inherits CC-BY-NC. "Text bridge" pipelines have a language model describe the video and pass the description to an off-the-shelf generator ([ACM survey, arXiv 2502.12489](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12489)). MSP is a text bridge: Claude is the describer, ACE-Step the generator. MSP also has an unfair advantage. The research models estimate scene cuts, impacts and "rhythm spots" from pixels, while MSP's builders know them to the frame. Three ideas are worth copying. Have the model reason step by step from visual facts to musical attributes before writing the prompt (CoT-VTM, [ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.647.pdf)). Treat emotion as a curve over time rather than one word ([EMSYNC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10154)). And split the work into describe → compose with rhythm control → assess and mix, as FilmComposer does ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Xie_FilmComposer_LLM-Driven_Music_Production_for_Silent_Film_Clips_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)). Products converge on the same small set of attributes. Adobe Firefly turns an uploaded video into an editable prompt of "vibe, style, purpose, energy, and tempo", with length equal to the video ([Adobe help](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/work-with-audio-and-video/work-with-audio/generate-soundtrack-for-an-uploaded-video.html)). ElevenLabs' composition plans give each section its own duration and styles ([ElevenLabs docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/music/composition-plans)). That is a useful model for MSP's brief format, but ElevenLabs is cloud-only.

The binding constraint is ACE-Step. **Duration is one global number; structure comes only from the order of tags in the lyrics field; no rule maps tags to seconds; and BPM, key and time signature are "guidance, not exact commands"** (ask for 120, get 118 or 122). Clips of 30–60 s are the stable range ([ACE-Step 1.5 Tutorial](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)). Users report extra length turning into "1+ minute long intros" and too much structure being ignored ([issue #391](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/issues/391)). Short forced clips spend about 4 s on a quiet intro, and takes end 2–5 s early ([rock3r/cav #99](https://github.com/rock3r/cav/issues/99); [ProsperoAI #36](https://github.com/quatrixone/ProsperoAI/issues/36)). Hence the rule already in the pipeline plan: ask for **video length × 1.2–1.3, never under about 20 s**, and let `fit.py` find the real drop and the real ending.

**The BPM arithmetic is simple and should be a script.** One 4/4 bar lasts 240/BPM seconds. The fitter starts the bar grid on `key`. For every BPM in the direction's range, the script measures how far each later anchor (punchline, `outro`, a section change) lands from the nearest downbeat, or beat for minor anchors. It keeps the BPM with the smallest weighted error, slightly favouring the house tempo. This is the film-scoring tempo finder, automated. Run on the real cue files, it reproduces the worked briefs. At **104 BPM** the museum reveal sits 32 beats (exactly 8 bars) after the pile-of-shame number, 11 ms off. At **121 BPM** Unlucky 13's section changes fall on +10 and +20 beats. At **123.7 or 98.9 BPM** the weekly Story's `outro` lands 5 or 4 bars after the poster drop, within 5 ms.

A catch: **a ±2 BPM wobble adds up.** At 102 instead of 104, the reveal 32 beats later arrives about 0.36 s late, which is far outside the "couple of frames" comedy needs. So `fit.py` must re-detect each candidate's actual tempo with beat_this, then either time-stretch it to the planned tempo or adjust the grid so the punchline and `outro` still land. Stretching by about 3 % or less is commonly treated as inaudible on beds, but that has no source and should be checked by ear once. Logging how far each anchor misses per fit will show how often stretching is needed.

When section placement keeps missing, there is a fallback ladder, cheapest first. Regenerate with a new seed. Use **Repaint** on the 3–90 s window around the planned drop with `[Drop - full]`. Or, untested, use **Cover mode** with a click-and-drum scaffold of the exact bar plan as `src_audio`. Community reports on Cover strength conflict, and one user got "something entirely different" ([Discussion #398](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/discussions/398)), so Cover needs one light lab evening before anyone relies on it. The 5 Hz LM planner should be off, or its metadata overridden, so it does not re-infer a different tempo.

The brief therefore has two halves. **Fields 1–6 are computed by a script from `cues.json` and the video duration. Fields 7–16 are written by Claude from the storyboard header, 3–6 keyframes and the standing feedback.** Claude must reason from visual facts to musical attributes in writing before filling the caption.

| # | Field | Who | What goes in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spotting table | script | time · event · music job (`establish`, `carry`, `hit`, `shift`, `stop-down`, `silence`, `drop`, `button`) · music or SFX; out point marked |
| 2 | Anchors | script | `key` (drop), punchline or reveal, `outro`, section changes, with the weights used |
| 3 | BPM and bars | script | chosen BPM, 4/4, bars from `key` to each anchor and its error in ms; runner-up BPM |
| 4 | Length | script | generate = video × 1.2–1.3, min ~20 s; hold for sticker |
| 5 | Stop-downs and ducks | script | where the fitter mutes (e.g. 0.3–0.6 s before a punchline) or dips 3–4 dB (logo sounds) |
| 6 | Fit instructions | script | drop → `key`; signature on the downbeat after `outro`; re-entry downbeat after a silence; last frame held |
| 7 | Story in one line + aura in 3 words | Claude | e.g. "cozy → dusty → mock-solemn" |
| 8 | Joke or reveal type | Claude | data reveal / confession (deadpan) / physical fail (cartoon) / launch / themed |
| 9 | Scoring stance + why | Claude | underscore, hit, or anempathetic counterpoint; match or readable mismatch |
| 10 | Emotional arc | Claude | 3–5 words in order, the energy curve per section |
| 11 | Direction + theme | Claude | house A data / B cozy / C playful, theme colour if any |
| 12 | Instruments | Claude | 3 named, each with a reason, from the aura table; house touch stated |
| 13 | Leitmotif / stinger | Claude | what object it marks, notes, when (built in the SFX layer, in the bed's key) |
| 14 | ACE caption, tags, mode | Claude | 8–12 comma keywords ending in `instrumental`; one concise tag per section in order; major/minor feel (key detected after generation) |
| 15 | Must not | Claude | standing no-gos (vocals, celebratory pop, ticking outside countdowns, recognisable tunes) plus post-specific ones |
| 16 | Ear check + risks | Claude | what the owner listens for; a variant if a tag may conflict |

ACE-Step's own guidance shapes field 14. Captions should be specific, free of conflicting styles, with BPM and key in parameters, not the caption ([ACE-Step Tutorial](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)). A third-party guide says to also write the BPM in the tags ([deAPI](https://deapi.ai/blog/ace-step-1-5-prompting-guide-how-to-write-tags-structure-lyrics-and-generate-better-music)), which is cheap to A/B. The ACE-Step repo ships its own Claude songwriting skill with a duration calculation ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/.claude/skills/acestep-songwriting/SKILL.md)). It is worth reading before the MSP brief prompt is fixed.

## Three briefs ready to run after the owner's go

The cue times come from the builders' cue files (`content/labs/FX1/src/shelf.cues.json`, `unlucky.cues.json`, `content/drafts/2026-11-16-weekly-rc1/stories/story-1-video.cues.json`). ### A. "I will solve it this weekend" (pile-of-shame Reel, 42.7 s)

The story: a shelf ages from DAY 1 to 1460 while the excuses pile up, the unsolved share lands, then a wall of boxes, then the joke that "Speed Puzzling" ends up in a museum. Aura: **cozy → dusty → mock-solemn**. Type: confession, deadpan. Stance: **play it straight and anempathetic**. A cozy, slightly dusty bed that doesn't notice the years passing makes the confession funnier. The museum gets a brief mock-solemn colour only *after* the reveal. Arc: cozy → creeping neglect → hush → mock-grand reveal → warm send-off.

| t (s) | Event | Music job |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.6 | splash on a new shelf, hook text | establish cozy; music already running, no intro silence |
| 3.1–11.55 | time-lapse, excuses at 3.9 / 6.45 / 9.0 / 11.55; sticker peel 6.4; spider 11.25 | carry an indifferent pulse; excuses = SFX; spider = stinger |
| ~13.6–14.4 | time-lapse ends | short stop-down or riser |
| **14.43** | `key`: share never solved | **drop**, gentle and cozy-bright |
| 15–29.6 | the wall, counter, "N days … snacks not included" | carry, lighter under reading |
| 31.8–32.9 | picture light flickers (32.3) | **silence** (fitter mutes) |
| **32.9** | `reveal`: museum | punchline in silence + one stinger; music re-enters on the next downbeat (~33.5) |
| **37.6** | `outro`: send line | resolve, falling; signature is the button |
| 41.7 | rewind loop | reverse whoosh SFX |

**BPM 104**, 4/4: reveal = +32 beats (8 bars) from `key`, 11 ms off; `outro` = +40 beats (10 bars), 96 ms off, which the fitter can absorb. The tempo finder's runner-up, 103.6, puts both anchors within 60 ms. The excuses, 2.55 s apart, do not lock to the grid. They stay on SFX. Mode: **major, wistful**; avoid sad minor. Generate **52–55 s**.

```
caption: lo-fi chamber pop, felt piano, pizzicato strings, music box, soft brushed drums, upright bass, cozy, wistful, deadpan, dusty vinyl texture, warm intimate mix, instrumental
tags:    [Intro - felt piano] [Verse - pizzicato] [Build] [Drop - warm] [Breakdown - sparse] [Bridge - solemn] [Outro - calm, descending] [Fade Out]
```

Leitmotif: the **spider** is a low pizzicato or bassoon three-note creep (scale steps 1-♭2-1) at 11.25, echoed as a single low note under the reveal. Must not: vocals, sad minor piano, horror strings (the spider is cute), ticking. Ear check: does the punchline land in real silence, and does the music feel unbothered during the time-lapse? Risk: `[Bridge - solemn organ]` would clash with the caption's instruments, so the tag is plain `[Bridge - solemn]`. MU3's R4-caught recipe (sneaky pizzicato and bassoon, 106 BPM) is the comparison seed if the owner wants a cheekier bed.

### B. "Unlucky 13" (Reel, 29.8 s)

The story: a stopwatch ticks past the hour while the last piece arrives the wrong way round, bonks, turns and snaps in 2 s late. Then the data: 13 near-miss seconds, Lucky vs Unlucky, a per-minute chart, a question. Aura: **ticking → crushed → wry**. Type: physical fail (cartoon), then data. Stance: **cartoon on the SFX, tense-then-relieved music**. The fail is hit by SFX within frames while the music *stops*. The data part is underscored. Arc: nervous → frantic → crushed silence → wry recount → warm question.

| t (s) | Event | Music job |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–3.4 | stopwatch 59:47 ticking; piece rises, bonk 2.7, turn 3.3 | tension: ticking pulse plus a held rising figure; bonk and jiggle = SFX |
| 3.4 | clock passes 1:00:00 | music **drops out** |
| 4.3 | snap, 2 s late | snap SFX in silence |
| 5.5 | "2 seconds over the hour" | deflated stinger (SFX), then the music re-enters lighter on the next bar |
| **8.83** | `key`: the near-miss count | **drop**, mid-energy |
| 13.8 / 18.8 | Lucky vs Unlucky / minute chart | shifts at +10 and +20 beats |
| **24.2** | `outro`: the ask | resolve, calm, falling; signature on the bar line (~25.3) |
| 28.8–29.8 | rewind loop | reverse SFX |

**BPM 121**, 4/4: shifts at +10.0 and +20.1 beats, `outro` at +31.0 beats, a one-beat pickup into the resolving bar, all within 40 ms. The opening clock is a separate SFX layer, not the bed. Mode: **major with "suspenseful"**. The fitter's dropout supplies the drama, because one caption can't reliably switch from minor to major. Generate **36–39 s**.

```
caption: cinematic tension pop, ticking clock pulse, pizzicato strings, marimba, muted trumpet, soft synth bass, light percussion, suspenseful, playful, wry, clean modern mix, instrumental
tags:    [Intro - tense ticking] [Build] [Verse - light] [Drop] [Bridge] [Outro - calm, descending] [Fade Out]
```

`[Silence]` is left out on purpose. ACE-Step can't time it, so the fitter's hard mute from 3.4 s does the job. Leitmotif: **the last piece** is a two-note rising "almost" (scale steps 5 → 6, unresolved) on the rise, the bonk and the turn. It resolves to the home note only on the late snap, so the resolution itself is the joke. An optional single celesta or harpsichord note adds Friday-13th colour. Must not: horror, the sad-trombone "wah-wah" cliché (owner to judge), vocals. Ear check: is the dropout before the snap clean, and does the data section feel wry rather than sad?

### C. Weekly Numbers Story (16.1 s cue sheet; 18 s version holds the end)

The story: the week's title card, seven day pieces landing, the poster with the week's headline number, count-ups, a box rain, the stopwatch, slot stops, then the cue to the full report. Aura: **bright, orderly, warm**. Type: data reveal. Stance: **underscore, stay congruent**. One drop on the poster, everything else SFX over a steady bed. Arc: anticipation → arrival → busy week → spotlight → calm thanks. This is a recurring format, so per the decision it keeps a **familiar base adapted to the week**. Change the aura words only when the week has a theme. Keep the direction, tempo and tags stable.

| t (s) | Event | Music job |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–0.75 | finished title card | music already playing, soft |
| 0.75–2.35 | day pieces land 1.3–2.12; busiest-day accent 2.35 | SFX plinks; bed builds |

## Rank candidates on hits first, sound quality second

Each of the 3–4 candidates goes through `fit.py` first, then gets a score card in its `.fit.json`. The owner hears them in rank order with the numbers beside each player. **Hard fails come first.** Vocals detected (`vocal_check.py`). Tempo off plan by more than about 4 %, unless at half or double time. A `key` miss of more than 40 ms after fitting. Clipping or loudness out of spec. Silence at the start. Bad drums or claps in the first 2 s (an MU1 note).

**The weighted score favours what the owner hears as "it hits".** First, *cue hit*: distance from `key`, the punchline and `outro` to the planned downbeat or silence. Second, *energy contour*: the bed's per-bar loudness and note density compared with the brief's arc, with a clear rise into `key` and a step down under the CTA. Third, *brief similarity*: LAION CLAP's music checkpoint scores the audio against the caption, with "vocals" and "singing" as penalty prompts. Use it as a relative ranking only, since no study validates CLAP against human taste for short beds. Last, a *quality tie-breaker*. Research systems use the same kinds of measure: CLAP scores, beat coverage, and cut-on-beat recall within 0.1 s ([ACM survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12489)). Claude can act as a second-stage judge, like FilmComposer's assessment agents. But it cannot listen, so give it the numbers and an energy-curve image, not a request for taste. Log the owner's pick to tune the weights. All of this runs on CPU or MPS in seconds per candidate and is **not a heavy job**.

The licences are clean if the tools are chosen carefully. **beat_this** has MIT code and weights. Its maintainer confirmed commercial use and redistribution are fine, and it avoids madmom's patented, non-commercial beat tracker ([CPJKU/beat_this](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this); [issue #21](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/21)). **LAION CLAP** is CC0, and its HF music model is Apache-2.0 ([LAION-AI/CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP); [model card](https://huggingface.co/laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech/blob/195c3a3e68faebb3e2088b9a79e79b43ddbda76b/README.md?code=true)). Prefer it over Microsoft CLAP, whose weights carry CC-BY-3.0 notice terms ([microsoft/CLAP](https://github.com/microsoft/CLAP)). **Avoid SongEval**: its README says non-commercial while its LICENSE file says Apache, a conflict ([ASLP-lab/SongEval](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/SongEval)).

**Open owner decision: Audiobox Aesthetics.** Meta's model scores production quality, complexity and enjoyment. It is licensed **CC-BY-4.0**, and Kandinsky 6.0's video pipeline uses it as a filter ([facebookresearch/audiobox-aesthetics](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiobox-aesthetics); [Kandinsky 6.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05608v1)). The owner's rule is **no credit or attribution anywhere** in posts, captions or profile. Audiobox never touches a post. It reads a candidate and outputs a few numbers, and nothing of it ends up in the music. The recommended reading follows the 16 Nov Magenta RT 2 precedent (CC-BY weights accepted because the outputs carry no obligation). **Allow it as an internal tie-breaker. Put its attribution in a repo notice (e.g. a third-party notices file) only, never in a post. Never commit or redistribute its weights.** That CC-BY's attribution duty attaches to sharing the model, not to using its scores, is this report's reading, not legal advice. If the owner prefers zero CC-BY in the toolchain, drop it. It is the lowest-weighted signal, and the ranker works without it.

## Conclusion

Tailoring is mostly about timing. The evidence ranks accent sync above tempo matching. The craft says to decide what the music does before choosing its genre. And the generator cannot place anything precisely. Together these point to one design: a script turns the cue sheet MSP already writes into a bar plan, Claude adds the feel, and the fitter makes the plan come true. Choosing the genre, the part that looks like composing, matters least. Stop-downs before punchlines, the drop on `key`, and the signature on the last downbeat are what the owner keeps hearing as "it hits".

The biggest unknown is not musical. Nobody has shown that a bed composed for each post earns more watch time or sends than a well-fitted library bed, and the costs differ: about 10–15 GPU minutes and an owner listening session per post, against none. That makes the insights log as important as the generator. If the scores and the owner's picks show the comic Reels gaining from a tailored bed and the weekly Story not, a reasonable split is: compose for the stories, keep a familiar base for the formats. That split is already in the 18 Nov decision.
