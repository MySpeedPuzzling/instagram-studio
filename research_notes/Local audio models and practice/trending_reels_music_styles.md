# Trending short-form music styles (2025 to Nov 2026) and ACE-Step 1.5 recipes for @myspeedpuzzling (research, 2026-11-18)

Scope: which music styles and audio trends work on Reels, Stories, TikTok and Shorts right now, and how to turn that into
instrumental ACE-Step 1.5 XL beds for MSP. This note builds on and does not repeat `house_sound_and_ace_prompting.md`
(caption format, tags, endings, the three house directions A "Clean data", B "Cozy table" and C "Playful pieces", the
signature motif) or `short_video_audio_best_practices.md` (tempo/persuasion study, sound-on share, loudness, phone bass,
first-second hook, loops).

Evidence labels:
- **[Platform]**: first-party data from TikTok, YouTube or Meta, or a charts/data company (Luminate, Chartmetric, IFPI) with a stated method
- **[Industry]**: music-library or agency trend writing (Epidemic Sound, Soundstripe, Musicbed) or third-party trend trackers; curated, no method given
- **[Opinion]**: practitioner or musicologist view, no data
- **[Inference]**: my reasoning from the above, not from any source

Research was done on 18 Nov 2026 with about 17 searches and fetches. Instagram's own "Trend Talk" / @creators reports did
not show up in search, so the Instagram side relies on third-party trackers (see Gaps).

## 1. What styles dominate trending Reels/TikTok audio in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
The charts that measure usage are dominated by **vocal songs**: nostalgia revivals (a 1962 song was TikTok's #1 of
2025), K-pop, Latin and global pop. MSP can't use or imitate those. Among instrumental and "non-verbal" audio, the
signals that hold up are:
1. **Brazilian phonk / funk**, including slowed versions. This is the strongest instrumental trend with platform data.
2. **Dance/electronic and house**, the fastest-growing US genre by share in H1 2026.
3. **Nu-disco / synth-funk**, used for before/after transitions.
4. **Library-style instrumentals**: hypnotic, cozy coffee, and slow-build "showdown" tracks.
5. **Evergreen comedic library tracks** such as "Monkeys Spinning Monkeys".

I found no 2025–26 platform data for lo-fi, Jersey club, hyperpop, amapiano or chiptune specifically.

- [Inference] For MSP, "trend" means borrowing **production textures** (phonk cowbell and 808 slides, disco/synth-funk
  bass, house four-on-the-floor, supersaw lifts), not songs. Those textures last much longer than any single sound.
- [Inference] Two trends match MSP's tone: nu-disco/synth-funk for before/after (in Instagram's own Oct 2026 trend
  pattern) and house/electronic loops (Luminate growth, loopable). Phonk matches only "speed" content and in a softened
  form. Nu-metal, pluggnB and drill don't fit a cozy-competitive puzzle brand.
- [Inference] The "organic sounds" call matches MSP's felt-piano and wood timbres, and is a reason not to make every bed
  glossy EDM.

## 2. Which styles fit which content formats?

### Takeaway
Content format matters more than trend for the style choice:
- **Satisfying/process videos**: popular ASMR content uses *less* music, so the bed should be quiet and let the
  foley/click lead.
- **Data/text-heavy Reels**: lyric-free, steady, low-interference beds (already covered in the earlier note).
- **Comedy**: a stable, decades-old set of **musical tropes** (circus/oom-pah bounce, sneaky pizzicato, frantic chase,
  overblown orchestral reveal, ironic lounge calm). These are evergreen, but the famous originals are copyrighted or
  need credit, so MSP needs trope-level descriptions that avoid each famous melody and lead instrument.
- **Countdown/hype**: slow builds and four-bar drops.

### Cited Findings
- **ASMR content uses less music [Moderate, older]**. Kovacevich & Huron (2019) compared 30 popular ASMR videos with
  controls: ASMR videos had "less music, decreased frame-size, and quiet, private scenes". The study describes content
  and does not test engagement. — [Empirical Musicology Review](https://emusicology.org/article/id/4569/)
- The "Oddly Satisfying" channel markets "original ASMR audio" as the selling point [Industry]. — [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCCZIevhN62jJ2gb-u__M95g)
- Process-heavy tutorials and routines are a rising Instagram format in Nov 2026 [Industry]. — [Lightreel](https://lightreel.ai/blogs/whats-trending-on-instagram)
- Text-heavy content suits "upbeat instrumental … where you want the message to shine with little audio interference"
  [Industry]. — [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/tiktok-sounds)
- Cozy coffee and morning-routine videos use smooth, warm instrumentals such as "Espresso Soul" [Industry]. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-songs/)
- Suspense and highlight content uses slow-build "showdown" instrumentals [Industry]. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-songs/)
- **Comedic tropes and copyright status**:
  - The silly bounce ("Monkeys Spinning Monkeys") draws on circus and Mozart references and a booming bass. — [GPB/NPR](https://www.gpb.org/news/2024/09/25/how-catchy-tune-became-the-soundtrack-tiktoks-silliest-videos)
  - The chase trope: "Yakety Sax" (Boots Randolph, 1963) became the Benny Hill closing chase, a send-up of the Keystone
    Cops. It is not public domain, and composers make royalty-free sound-alikes for that reason. — [Wikipedia: Yakety Sax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakety_Sax); [Orion Williams sound-alike note](http://toolofgod.com/my-music/royalty-free-benny-hill-theme-style-alternative/)
  - Kevin MacLeod's "Investigations" appears as a "similar track" to Monkeys Spinning Monkeys in the Funny/Comedy
    category. I found no usage data. — [Chosic](https://www.chosic.com/download-audio/27247/)
- Afrobeats/reggaeton-style beds at 100–115 BPM sit well behind voiceover [Industry]. House beds "keep energy under
  dialogue" [Industry]. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/royalty-free-music-top-trends)

### Inferences
- [Inference] **Puzzling time-lapse / "satisfying" footage**: the bed sits low (about −20 to −24 LUFS short-term under
  the foley), sparse and slow-moving (lo-fi or ambient pluck). Piece clicks and box-pour sounds carry the hook, and a
  "no music, foley only" variant is worth A/B testing.
- [Inference] **Comedy works by contrast with the picture**. Calm bossa over chaos (a pile of shame) and epic orchestra
  over something tiny (finding the last piece) are juxtaposition jokes, and they need the music to start at the joke's
  setup, not under the punchline. Circus bounce and sneaky pizzicato are "matching" jokes for clumsy or guilty
  behaviour.
- [Inference] The trope matters, not the melody. Each comedic recipe below changes the lead instrument away from the
  famous original (no tenor sax lead for the chase, no whistle plus ukulele for the bounce, no chromatic saxophone line
  for sneaky).

### Gaps
- No data comparing comedic music tropes on engagement. Everything in this section beyond the ASMR content analysis is
  practitioner or musicologist opinion.
- No study of music vs no music on satisfying/process Reels specifically.

## 3. What makes a track feel "viral/modern" in short-form?

### Takeaway
The data supports **speed of trend decay**, **loopability**, and **"repeatable action" audio**. It does not support
specific tempos or "drop = virality". The most modern-sounding production signals in 2025–26 are slowed or half-time
phonk weight, house/disco grooves, supersaw/vocal-chop-style lifts with a four-bar drop, and "organic" real-instrument
warmth. Hook, loudness, phone bass and loop endings are covered in the earlier note and not repeated here.

### Cited Findings
- **Trends move faster and leave less behind [Platform, Chartmetric]**. Average days for a hit to reach 100k TikTok posts
  fell from about 340 (2020) to about 48 (2025). Spotify streams per TikTok post fell from about 738 to about 275. The
  sample is 49 songs per year (about 299 tracks) and the authors call it "an early indication". — [Chartmetric, 7 Nov 2025](https://hmc.chartmetric.com/why-tiktok-songs-go-viral-faster/)
- TikTok's share of music listening on social video apps fell from 34% to 29% in 2024, and from 51% to 42% among
  13–24-year-olds (MusicWatch, as reported). — [Music 3.0](https://music3point0.com/2025/02/26/what-if-i-told-you-that-tiktok-is-losing-popularity-with-music-users/)
- **"Repeatable action" sounds beat background music [Industry]**: in August 2026, the strongest new TikTok sounds were
  tied to repeatable actions rather than background use. — [Lightreel TikTok weekly](https://www.lightreel.ai/blogs/whats-trending-on-tiktok)
- "Danceable, loop-ready audio" and non-verbal tracks drove Shorts in 2025 [Industry, unsourced claim]. — [MilX](https://milx.app/en/trends/video-content-trends-for-2026-on-youtube-and-social-media)
- Building a brand's own short sonic identity (a "ta-dum" for TikTok) is listed as a 2026 audio prediction [Opinion]. — [deeka.ai](https://deeka.ai/blog/trending-tiktok-sounds-2026)
- Listeners and buyers increasingly search music by mood descriptors ("warm", "slow build", "tense", "no vocals"), and
  Spotify expanded its prompt playlists in Jan 2026 [Industry]. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/royalty-free-music-top-trends)
- The sped-up remix beat its original about 11× in usage for "Kill Bill" (older, peak era). — [Audio Network](https://blog.audionetwork.com/the-edit/music/the-rise-of-sped-up-music/)
- "Monkeys Spinning Monkeys" is "musically unexceptional" by its own composer's account and spread by imitation.
  Catchiness plus ubiquity mattered more than sophistication [Opinion]. — [GPB/NPR](https://www.gpb.org/news/2024/09/25/how-catchy-tune-became-the-soundtrack-tiktoks-silliest-videos)

### Inferences
- [Inference] "Viral feel" for an own-music account comes from four things:
  1. A **2–4 note motif** stated in bar 1 and repeated (earworm through repetition, not complexity).
  2. A **four-bar build into a drop** aligned to the key visual moment.
  3. **Groove you can nod to** (four-on-the-floor or half-time 808).
  4. A **loop seam**.

  Vocals and lyrics, the main driver of chart virality, are off the table, so these are the levers left.
- [Inference] The "sped-up" sound can be had without the nightcore chipmunk effect: generate at the target BPM, then
  optionally pitch the finished bed +1 to +2 semitones (or +3 to +5% speed) for brightness. Don't do a full 1.25× nightcore
  shift, which sounds dated for a brand. The "slowed + bass" (phonk) feel is the 2025–26 counterpart: half-time drums
  under a faster hi-hat grid.
- [Inference] "Repeatable action" matters most for MSP. If each bed has a very short, recognisable sting (the MSP
  signature) at the same event (rank reveal, piece click), the sound becomes "the MSP sound" across posts. That is the
  closest an account with baked-in music can get to owning a trending sound.

### Gaps
- No platform data on BPM ranges or drop placement versus completion or shares for Reels or TikTok.
- No 2025–26 data on whether sped-up or nightcore audio is still rising.

## 4. Trend lifecycle: chase trending sounds or go evergreen?

### Takeaway
For an account that bakes its own music, chasing specific sounds is both impossible (you can't use the sound) and
pointless: a trend reaches peak in about 7 weeks, and its pull comes from the *recognisable recording*, which a style
imitation doesn't carry. The best strategy is an **evergreen house core** (the A/B/C directions plus the signature),
with **"seasonal flavour" beds** drawn from durable genre textures (house, disco/synth-funk, soft phonk, festive), refreshed
about quarterly. Put the reach effort into the *video format* and the hook, not into the music trend.

### Cited Findings
- About 48 days to 100k posts in 2025, and lower downstream conversion. — [Chartmetric](https://hmc.chartmetric.com/why-tiktok-songs-go-viral-faster/)
- Trend pages show that whole sound charts turn over within weeks. For example, Instagram's mid-November chart is
  holiday songs, while October's was "Upside Down" and "Last Train to London". An old song can regain momentum when
  attached to a dance, edit or fandom narrative. — [Lightreel](https://lightreel.ai/blogs/whats-trending-on-instagram); [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/free-tools/trending-instagram-audio/)
- Soundstripe warns that nostalgia trends are tied to specific recordings that can't be licensed, and suggests using
  the reproducible production elements instead [Industry]. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/royalty-free-music-top-trends)
- Genre-level trends last longer. Brazilian phonk was still growing in 2026 after the 2021 drift-phonk start ("Close
  Eyes"), and dance/electronic gained share over a half-year. — [Billboard Brasil](https://billboard.com.br/brazilian-phonk-crescimento-global-tiktok-funk/); [Wikipedia: Close Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Eyes); [Soundstripe/Luminate](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/royalty-free-music-top-trends)
- The earlier note found only weak, non-primary claims that Instagram favours original audio. — `short_video_audio_best_practices.md` §4

### Inferences
- [Inference] Recommended split per month: about 70% evergreen house beds (recognition), about 20% "flavour" beds tied
  to a durable genre texture (house, disco, soft phonk, festive), and about 10% experimental. Log
  `music_direction` / `recipe_id` per post so the insights pipeline can compare them. MSP's own numbers will matter more
  than any trend report at ~2700 followers.
- [Inference] Seasonal is the one "trend" worth timing. December (Advent campaign) calls for a festive flavour (sleigh
  bells, celesta, pizzicato, swing), but **never a carol melody** that is still under copyright. Public-domain carols are
  legally fine, but model output can drift toward famous modern arrangements.
- [Inference] Revisit the flavour list every quarter with one trend-tracker pass, at the genre level only.

### Gaps
- No data on how original audio from small brand accounts performs versus trending library sounds on Instagram.

## 5. ACE-Step 1.5 recipes mapped to MSP post types

### Takeaway
There are 12 recipes. Six are evergreen (house directions refined for specific formats plus the comedic tropes) and six
lean on a trend or a flavour. Format, tags and fitting follow the earlier note: comma-list captions, BPM/key as
parameters, ask for 20–30% more duration, detect the real key after generation, and let `fit.py` put the `[Drop]` on
the key cue. Every caption ends with `instrumental`, and the lyrics field holds tags only.

### Cited Findings
- Sources for the trend basis are cited per recipe below. Caption and tag conventions come from
  `house_sound_and_ace_prompting.md` §1. The owner's MU1 likes (data electronic, cozy lo-fi, playful pizzicato/marimba)
  and dislikes (celebratory pop; ticking only for countdowns) are in `docs/ideas/music-style-system.md`.

### Inferences (recipes)
Legend: **E** = evergreen, **T** = trend-dependent (refresh or retire after about 3–6 months). Evidence = strength of the
*style-fits-format* claim, not of performance. **Copy risk** = risk of sounding like a specific copyrighted song.

**R1. "Disco flip" (before/after, weekly recap Reel)**: T, evidence Industry (Instagram Oct 2026 disco before/after
transitions; Epidemic Nu Disco/Synth Funk playlists). Copy risk: medium. Don't prompt "Upside Down", Chic or "Nile
Rodgers". Avoid a funky-guitar plus string-stab intro that copies a famous one.
- Caption: `nu disco, synth funk, slap bass groove, funky clean guitar chops, four on the floor, string stabs, soft pluck synth, bright, groovy, confident, polished modern mix, instrumental`
- Tags: `[Intro - bass and hats]` `[Build - filter rising]` `[Drop - full groove]` `[Breakdown - bass only]` `[Outro - calm, descending]`
- 118–122 BPM, major (Dorian also works). Mood: groovy, sunny, confident. Drop on the "after" frame.

**R2. "House loop" (rankings and data Reels that loop)**: T/E, evidence Platform-ish (Luminate dance/electronic growth)
plus Industry (house loops cleanly under text). Copy risk: low.
- Caption: `melodic house, deep house groove, warm sub bass, plucked synth riff, felt piano chords, crisp claps, shaker, woodblock accent, uplifting, smooth, loopable, instrumental`
- Tags: `[Intro - plucks]` `[Build]` `[Drop]` `[Breakdown - light]` `[Drop]` `[Outro - loop back to intro]`
- 120–124 BPM, minor-to-relative-major feel (e.g. A minor with C major lifts). Mood: smooth, hypnotic, forward.
- For loop Reels, fit the end so the last bar leads into bar 1 rather than using the descending outro.

**R3. "Soft phonk sprint" (speed records, fastest solves, "under 30 minutes" Reels)**: T, evidence Platform (Brazilian
phonk +62% in Europe 2026; "Passo Bem Solto (Slowed)" was #2 on US Shorts 2025). Copy risk: medium-high. The genre is
built on a few hit cowbell melodies. Ask for an *original* melody on marimba or pluck, not cowbell, and never prompt
"Passo Bem Solto", "Close Eyes" or "Murder in My Mind". Keep it unthreatening: no dark Memphis vocal chops, and keep the
bass clean.
- Caption: `soft phonk, brazilian funk rhythm, punchy 808 bass slides, crisp hi-hats, marimba hook, plucked synth, energetic, determined, playful edge, clean modern mix, instrumental`
- Tags: `[Intro - marimba hook]` `[Build - hats rolling]` `[Drop - 808 and funk drums]` `[Outro - calm, descending]`
- 128–135 BPM (or around 140 with a half-time feel). Minor mode, but bright via the marimba. Mood: driven, fast, cheeky.

**R4. "Caught in the act" (pile of shame, confessions, guilty secrets)**: E, evidence Opinion (classic comedic
"sneaky" trope; Kevin MacLeod "Investigations" is in that cluster). Copy risk: medium. Avoid a chromatic tenor-sax
walking line, which points straight at the Pink Panther theme. Use pizzicato and bassoon instead.
- Caption: `sneaky comedic orchestral, pizzicato strings, bassoon, tiptoe staccato, muted trumpet hits, woodblock, light snare brushes, mischievous, guilty, playful, cartoon tension, instrumental`
- Tags: `[Intro - tiptoe pizzicato]` `[Verse - sneaky]` `[Build - tension]` `[Drop - comic hit]` `[Outro - descending, sheepish]`
- 100–112 BPM, minor key with a major ending (the "reveal" lands on a cheeky major chord). Mood: sneaky, guilty, funny.
- This is house direction C pushed toward comedy. It is the first one to test, since pile of shame is MSP's best performer.

**R5. "Bouncy hobby chaos" (relatable meme Reels: "me buying another puzzle")**: E, evidence Platform-ish (the "Monkeys
Spinning Monkeys" type is among the most-used sounds on TikTok, per NPR 2024, older). Copy risk: medium. Avoid the
whistle plus ukulele plus glockenspiel lead combination and its specific bass line. Use marimba and tuba oom-pah
instead, with a swing feel.
