# Track 4: What works. Giveaways, advent and daily-challenge campaigns in niche communities

Research for "Advent of Puzzle" (Dec 2026) on @myspeedpuzzling. Compiled 2026-10-17.
Labels: **[E]** evidence (dataset, study, dated report or case with numbers), **[V]** vendor/blog claim without a
method, **[I]** inference or opinion (mine). Where the evidence is thin, this file says so.

---

## Takeaway

1. **Daily-challenge participation halves in about 4–6 days, then levels off.** In Advent of Code 2022–2024
   (~290–344k people on day 1), 66–83 % came back for day 2, 28–39 % for day 7, 14–18 % for day 14 and 5–9 % for day 25.
   Between days 2 and 8 each day kept 80–89 % of the day before; after the midpoint it kept 90–99 % [E].
   AoC puzzles get harder every day, so a light daily quiz should lose people more slowly [I].
2. **People who get through the first half mostly finish.** In a 24-door school advent calendar (KI-Adventskalender,
   2024–25), the steepest drop was day 1→2, and **>76 % of people active on day 12 finished day 24** [E].
   Advent of Code shows the same flattening after day ~12 [E].
3. **Easy daily habits keep most people.** Strava's "Days Active" challenges had a **79 % completion rate** [E/V: Strava's
   own figure, method not shown]. On a normal day only **~15 % of Wordle players break their streak** [E: NYT talk at GDC,
   reported by PC Gamer]. The quiz difficulty decides which curve we get [I].
4. **Giveaway followers leave.** In the only measured case (52 medical accounts, 6 sponsored "loop" giveaways, 2019–20),
   **61 % of new followers unfollowed within 2 weeks** [E]. Blogs quote 60–80 % gone within 30 days for generic
   "follow to enter" giveaways [V], and 78–82 % kept when the prize is niche or branded [V]. A voucher for puzzlers is
   niche, so we should land at the better end, but **we have to measure it ourselves** [I].
5. **Stories mostly reach existing followers, so a Story quiz keeps the audience but won't grow it.** Stories are shown
   only to followers (Mosseri, Apr 2026) [E]. Story reach for accounts with 1K–5K followers is **~3–10 % of followers per
   Story** (Socialinsider 2025, 161k Stories; the report gives both figures) [E]. For us that is roughly **70–250 viewers per
   quiz Story** [I].
6. **Few people reply to a Story by DM; sticker taps are more common.** Measured median Story replies were **~0.3–0.5 per
   1,000 impressions** (Rival IQ, 1,470 brands, 2023), and that was about half the year before [E]. Blogs put quiz-sticker
   responses at 6–15 % of viewers [V]. With a prize attached, I would plan on **~10–60 DM answers per day in week 1** [I].
7. **The feed is where the reach is, but too many giveaway posts can hurt the whole account.** The Recommendation
   Guidelines exclude content that "promotes a contest or giveaway" from recommendations to non-followers [E: the guideline
   text, as quoted by Buffer]. Mosseri named "giving out too many giveaways" as something that can make an account
   not recommendable at all (reported paraphrase, Feb 2025) [V]. **24 giveaway feed posts is the riskiest option** [I].
8. **Posting more often doesn't lower reach per post.** In Buffer's study (2.1M posts, 102k accounts, each account
   compared with itself), 6–9 posts a week grew followers about 1.7× faster than 3–5 a week, and reach per post was
   12–24 % *higher* for frequent posters [E]. So a month of daily Stories on top of ~4 feed posts a week is fine, as long
   as the quality holds [I].
9. **Rigged-draw accusations follow hidden draws.** In Justin Sun's 2019 Tesla draw, the picker log showed **88 runs**
   before the winner was announced, and the backlash cost him a second car [E]. RANDOM.ORG's third-party draws
   (**$4.95 per draw up to 500 entries**) leave a record that can't be edited, so a re-run shows up [E].
10. **Fake "you won" DMs are common.** Scams that started on social media cost **$2.1B in 2025, ~30 % of all
    reported scam losses** (FTC, Apr 2026), and Instagram came third among platforms [E]. Brands and creators
    answer with a fixed line: "we never ask for payment, winners are contacted only from @official" [E: dated examples].

## 1. Advent and daily-challenge campaigns: participation, drop-off, follower effects

### 1.1 The participation curve (Advent of Code is the best public dataset)

- **What was measured:** the official stats pages count everyone who solved part 1 or both parts of each day,
  **including people who catch up later**. That makes them a ceiling on "participated that day" [E].
  The AoC numbers in this section were computed from the 2022–2025 pages on 2026-10-17.
- **Share of day 1 still taking part** [E]:

  | Day | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 (12-day) |
  |----|------|------|------|------|
  | 2 | 83 % | 66 % | 82 % | 68 % |
  | 3 | 71 % | 48 % | 66 % | 59 % |
  | 6 | 53 % | 33 % | 40 % | 41 % |
  | 7 | 39 % | 28 % | 33 % | 35 % |
  | 12 | 21 % | 15 % | 21 % | 11 % |
  | 14 | 17 % | 14 % | 18 % | — |
  | 20 | 8 % | 8 % | 11 % | — |
  | 24 | 6 % | 6 % | 10 % | — |
  | 25 | 7 % | 5 % | 9 % | — |

- **Share of the previous day kept:** usually 80–89 % in days 2–8, 85–100 % in the second half. Some later days
  *gain* people (catch-up after a hard day; days 18–25 in 2022 and 2024) [E]. Synopsys' analysis found the largest
  drops in the first seven days, and that "only 10 % of participants ever make it to the end" [E].
- **Why people drop:** puzzles get harder (4+ hours later in the month), plus holiday and year-end work [V: BigGo, Oct
  2025]. People reacting to the 12-day change said they usually fall off around days 7–12 anyway [V: HN thread].
  The organiser shortened the event because of his own workload, not because of the audience [E: Wastl, Oct 2025].
- **A hard day 1 costs the whole month.** AoC 2023's day 1 was seen as hard. It lost 34 % on day 2 (versus
  17–18 % in 2022 and 2024) and stayed lower all month (5 % vs 7–9 % on day 25) [E]. **Day 1 should be easy** [I].
- **KI-Adventskalender** (24 doors, school students, sponsored prizes, a week to answer each door) [E]: participation
  grew in its second year (+47 % on day 1, +25 % finishing day 24). Its drop-off had the same shape as AoC's: steep
  d1→2, then **>76 % of day-12 actives finished**.
- **Advent of Chess** (24 doors, open 24 h, every correct answer = one ticket for a signed board): 2,232 people
  on the leaderboard, prize draw among everyone listed [E]. It is the closest analogue to our mechanic. It doesn't
  publish a curve or a draw method [E: gap].

### 1.2 Lighter, habit-type challenges keep more people

- Strava Days Active: 79 % completion [E/V]. Strava annual goals: 72 % of runners who set a 2024 goal hit it [V: Runify,
  citing Strava Year in Sport].
- Wordle: ~15 % of players break their streak on a normal day, 60 % on a very hard word [E]. Difficulty spikes break
  streaks [E].
- Puzzle mobile games: about 32 % / 12 % / 5 % of players still active on days 1 / 7 / 30 [V: Segwise 2026].
- Duolingo: a streak wager raised day-7 retention by 14 %, and a weekend streak freeze made users 4 % more likely to
  come back. Users who binge are more likely to quit [E: Duolingo blog]. A former growth head said a lost streak is "a big
  reason why people quit" [V].
- **What this means for us:** a 30-second data quiz is much closer to Wordle or Days Active than to AoC [I].
  A reasonable expectation is to keep **~50–70 % of week-1 Monday answerers through Saturday**. AoC's ~33 % on day 7 is
  the worst case [I].

### 1.3 Niche hobby examples (thin evidence)

- Puzzle brands mostly *sell* advent calendars (Ravensburger, Galison, NYPC, Wentworth, Artifact, SpeedCubeShop).
  None of them publishes engagement numbers for an Instagram advent [E: product pages 2025–26].
- Ravensburger DE runs a **website** advent: a puzzle in each door, a draw among *that day's* entrants, e-mail
  notification. It has run every year since at least ~2020 [E format]. That it keeps running suggests it pays off [I].
- BGG's community advent (play a game every day, no prize) peaked at **128 participants** and then flattened [E]. A
  no-prize daily challenge in a niche stays small but loyal [I].
- No cubing brand (GAN, MoYu, SpeedCubeShop, Cubelelo) was found running an Instagram daily-scramble advent [E: search
  gap]. A small "Puzzle Advent Calendar" Instagram account (~758 followers) posts a creator's puzzle a day, with no
  numbers [E].

### 1.4 Follower effects, unfollows and fatigue

- **Unfollows after giveaways:** 61 % within 2 weeks, and a 71 % chance that more than half leave (sponsored loop
  giveaways, 2019–20) [E]. Blogs give a wide range: 60–80 % lost in 30 days without follow-up content
  [V: AiGrow, brandID], 78–82 % kept for niche or branded prizes versus 18–22 % for cash or gift cards [V: brandID], and
  "70–95 % kept" [V, no method].
- **Our numbers are already better than a loop giveaway:** the WJPC giveaway brought 60 follows from 4,357 reach
  (1.4 %) [E own]. Those are people who came to a puzzling event, not compers [I]. **Gap: we haven't measured how many of
  them stayed after 30 days.** Do that before December.
- **Compers** (people who enter giveaways as a hobby) are told to follow *niche* brands because there are fewer
  entrants [E: SuperLucky]. A voucher with a quiz that needs puzzling knowledge filters most of them out [I].
- **Fatigue:** no study measures day-by-day decline in an Instagram advent [E: gap]. The closest evidence is that 46 %
  of consumers would unfollow a brand over *too many promotional* messages (Sprout Q3 2016). That is about promotion,
  not posting frequency [E]. Buffer found no reach penalty for posting more often [E].
- **How much daily posting is fine:** Buffer (Aug 2025): extra follower growth per week was +0.12 % at 1–2 posts,
  +0.26 % at 3–5, +0.44 % at 6–9 and +0.66 % at 10+. Weeks with no posts did worse [E]. Mosseri's 2021 baseline was
  "two feed posts a week, two Stories a day" [V: secondary]. Brands in the 1K–5K tier post ~12 Stories a month
  [E: Socialinsider]. One Story a day for 24 days is ~2× our tier's norm and below what Mosseri suggested [I].

## 2. Feed posts vs Stories for daily quizzes

### 2.1 Reach

- Stories are shown only to followers, and feed reaches more people; reposting a feed post to Stories "is not going
  to meaningfully change your reach" (Mosseri, Stories Q&A, reported 6 Apr 2026) [E].
- Socialinsider 2025 (161,180 Stories, Jan–May 2024/25) [E]:
  - Reach as a share of followers is highest for small accounts. The 1K–5K tier shows **9.55 % (image) / 10.40 %
    (video)** in the format table but **3.30 %** in the Stories-vs-feed table, where feed is 4.00 %. The two
    tables don't match.
  - Exit rate for 1K–5K accounts: 12.8 % (image) / 13.4 % (video). Tap-forward: 56.8 % (image) / 50.5 % (video).
- Rival IQ 2024 (1,470 brands, Jun–Nov 2023) [E]: exit rate ~14 % on single-frame Stories, falling to ~5 % by 5 frames
  a day; median tap-forward 89 %; retention stays above 75 % through 7 frames a day; median replies roughly halved
  year on year.
- Completion rates vary a lot by vendor and definition: Dash Social 68–70 %, Bazaarvoice 82 %, Flick 87 %, "90 %+ for
  micro accounts" [V]. **Best used as a range, ~70–90 %** [I].

### 2.2 Replies and stickers

- What Mosseri has said about Story ranking: Instagram predicts "how likely you are to tap into a story, reply to a
  story in DMs or move on" to order the Stories tray [E: quoted by Buffer]. So replies help the *tray position* for
  people who already follow us, not reach to new people [I; one blog reports Mosseri saying DMs don't affect wider
  distribution, V].
- Measured reply rate: medians of ~0.27–0.48 replies per 1,000 impressions, top quartile ~0.8–1.35 [E: Rival IQ].
  Those Stories didn't ask for a reply. A prize plus a clear "reply with your answer" should do far better, but
  there is no benchmark [I].
- Sticker responses: quiz 6–15 %, poll 8–20 %, all interactive stickers 12–25 % of viewers [V; Rival IQ notes the API
  hides sticker data, so there is no audited dataset].
- **DM replies are the hardest ask** (typing an answer) but give us a name and a timestamp. Quiz stickers are easier but
  harder to export [I]. **Track 1/2 must check what the API returns for both.**
- Non-follower replies go to **Message Requests**, and templated high-volume messages can land in **Hidden
  Requests/Spam** [V: inro, instantdm 2026]. **We could miss entries** unless someone checks both folders daily [I].
- The Graph API stops returning Requests threads after 30 days without activity [V], so answers have to be collected
  daily [I].

### 2.3 The advent calendar Story highlight

- Common advice is one Story door a day plus a pinned "Advent" highlight, sometimes with a feed grand-prize post
  [V: Easypromos, Fastory, Facelift]. **No published numbers** on whether the highlight drives late joiners [E: gap].
- My view: a highlight is cheap and lets latecomers see the rules and past doors. Since answers close when the
  Story expires, the highlight is for rules and winners, not for answering late [I].

### 2.4 Do 24 daily feed posts tire followers?

- No direct evidence. Buffer finds no reach penalty at 10+ posts a week [E]. But each giveaway feed post isn't
  recommended to non-followers, and "too many giveaways" can cost the whole account its recommendations [E guideline;
  V Mosseri paraphrase]. **The danger isn't follower fatigue, it's our normal posts losing their reach** [I].
- Our WJPC giveaway still reached ~70 % non-followers [E own]. Either shares and tags spread it, or the classifier
  didn't flag that post. **One good result doesn't show that 24 giveaway posts are safe** [I].

## 3. Winner announcement, trust and anti-scam

### 3.1 Draw methods, from most to least verifiable

1. **Commit-reveal with a public random beacon:** before entries close, publish a hash of a secret seed and the future
   Bitcoin block (or another public random value) that will be used. After the draw, reveal the seed. Anyone can
   recompute the winner from the seed, the beacon and the entry list [E: The Raffle Shop, bitcoindata.science,
   conduition.io]. Mistake to avoid: a `last 6 hex digits % n` shortcut adds a small bias; hash the full value [I].
   It's very transparent but hard for non-technical followers to read, so pair it with a simple explanation [I].
2. **RANDOM.ORG Third-Party Draw Service:** **$4.95 per draw up to 500 entries**. The record (owner, UTC time,
   entrants, winners) can't be modified or deleted and stays for ≥5 years. Records can be *public*, *private* or
   *entrant-accessible* (entrants check their own ID without the list being shown) [E]. Each draw is a separate dated
   record, so a re-draw shows up in public records [E/I]. **Entrant-accessible mode avoids publishing usernames**,
   which fits our privacy rules [I].
3. **Live or screen-recorded draw with a comment picker:** the vendors themselves recommend recording the draw,
   posting the entrant count, and drawing backup winners at the same time [V: ExportComments, Pick Comment, Spin a Name].
   Weakness: a recording doesn't prove the tool wasn't run several times before. The **Justin Sun case**: 88 logged
   draws, the winner on screen before the "draw", a re-draw, and finally two cars [E].
   Some free pickers read only the last ~150 comments [V], which can leave out real entrants [I].
4. **Picking by hand:** looks rigged even when it isn't [V].

- A vendor reports that the most common accusation is "there was no draw", not "the draw was rigged" [V: ZocialComment].
  The fix is to publish **the number of valid entries, when the draw happened and how** [V/I].
- Promotion rules: publish official rules (eligibility, dates with time zone, how to enter, how and when the winner is
  picked) and include the Instagram release ("not sponsored, endorsed or administered by... Instagram") [E: Meta Promotion
  Guidelines, as summarised by Woobox and ShortStack]. Guides disagree on whether "follow to enter" is allowed [V].
  **Not requiring a follow fixes this**, and since non-followers' Story replies go to Requests, a follow requirement
  adds little anyway [I].

### 3.2 Impersonation and fake winner DMs

- Scale: **$2.1B lost to scams that started on social media in 2025, ~30 % of everyone who reported a loss**, eight
  times 2020 (FTC Data Spotlight, Apr 2026). Instagram came third behind Facebook and WhatsApp [E].
- How the scams work: look-alike handles (an extra letter, a `_giveaway` suffix), copied posts, blocking the real account
  so it's harder to report, and asking for "shipping fees", gift cards, crypto, card details or a link or app to "claim"
  the prize [E: Axios 2021; author Becca J. Ross; FTC]. Fake accounts appear exactly when a giveaway is running [E: cases].
- How brands protect participants, in dated examples [E]: "We will NEVER ask you to send money… payment, gift cards, or
  cryptocurrency… Winners are ONLY contacted through our official channel" (Yota Xpedition, 2026). "I will always
  announce the winner on my own account… then it will be up to you to DM me" (Becca J. Ross).
- **Best practice** [I, from the cases]: (a) put a fixed anti-scam line in every rules post and every winner Story;
  (b) **announce the winner publicly first and let the winner DM us**, rather than us DMing them first, so a stranger's
  "you won" DM is fake by definition; (c) never use links, forms, logins or fees to claim; (d) check daily for
  look-alike accounts during the campaign and report them; (e) pin the rules in the highlight.

## Design implications for Advent of Puzzle

All [I] unless marked. Dates: 1st Advent Sunday is **29 Nov 2026**; the other Advent Sundays are 6, 13 and 20 Dec;
Christmas Eve is a Thursday.

**Calendar.** Four Mon–Sat weeks ending on Advent Sundays: **23–28 Nov (draw 29 Nov), 30 Nov–5 Dec (draw 6 Dec),
7–12 Dec (draw 13 Dec), 14–19 Dec (draw 20 Dec)**. This avoids Christmas week, when AoC-type attention is lowest and
holiday commitments bite [V]. The option starting 30 Nov puts week 4 into 21–26 Dec; if chosen, shorten week 4 to
21–23 Dec.

**Expected participation curve.** Let N = day-1 DM answers (my guess ~15–60, from ~70–250 Story views × a reply rate
nobody has published).
- Week 1: d2 ≈ 75–85 % of N, Sat ≈ 50–70 % of N if the quizzes stay easy (AoC worst case ≈ 33 %).
- Weeks 2–4: a stable core of ~35–55 % of N plus newcomers. Expect a small **Monday bump after each Sunday winner
  Story** (a public winner proves the draw is real), and a dip on hard days.
- Plan on the people active in week 2 staying to the end, as in KI-Adventskalender (>76 % of mid-point actives) [E].

**Where to put the weight.** (1) **Day 1 and the launch carousel**: easiest question, clearest rules, best visual,
because d1→2 is the biggest drop everywhere [E]. (2) **Each Monday**: a fresh start for latecomers, with last week's
winner in the first frame. (3) **The Sunday draw Story**: proof, entry count, anti-scam line. (4) Saturdays fit our
weekend-heavy community, so Saturday can be the "harder" or bonus question. Avoid hard spikes on weekdays: Wordle's
PARER broke 60 % of streaks [E].

**Mechanic.** Weekly draws among correct answerers, entries resetting each week, so people who start in week 3 still
have a real chance. Optional extra: one bonus ticket for answering all 6 days (streak) [E: Duolingo]. **No follow
required to enter**: it avoids the gray area in the rules, and our own content does the following-conversion better.

**Story vs feed.** Daily quizzes go in **Stories**, which keeps contest content out of the feed's recommendation
pipeline and serves current followers. Use **one feed post a week at most** for the campaign (launch carousel; then
weekly recap and winner, written as a *data story* about how people answered, not as a giveaway ad). Keep the normal
~4 feed posts a week unchanged; Buffer shows no reach penalty for the extra Stories [E]. Daily feed giveaway posts
risk account-level recommendability [V].

**Answers.** Collect DMs **daily** from Primary, General, **Requests and Hidden Requests/Spam** before the Story expires
and before 30-day API blind spots [V]. Ask for a short, distinct answer format (a number or a letter) so manual
checking is quick. Keep in mind that templated messages may be filtered into Hidden Requests.

**Announcement and trust.**
- Before the campaign: publish rules (eligibility, time zone CET, how to enter, draw method, voucher terms, Instagram
  release) in a pinned highlight and on a page on myspeedpuzzling.com.
- Draw: **RANDOM.ORG third-party draw in entrant-accessible mode** ($4.95 a week, ~$20 total) with the record link in
  the winner Story. Optionally also publish a hash of the anonymised entry list before the draw (commit-reveal light).
- Winner Story: number of valid entries, how many answered correctly, UTC draw time, record link, winner's handle
  (with consent and after checking do-not-feature, private profile and ranking opt-out), backup winner drawn at the
  same time.
- **The winner DMs us**, not the other way round.

**Anti-scam line** (in the rules, every winner Story and the highlight):
> "We announce winners only here on @myspeedpuzzling. We never ask for payment, card details, logins or links to
> 'claim' a prize, and we never message from any other account. If someone else contacts you, it's a scam: please
> report it."

**Measure and compare.** Day-by-day answer counts, Story views and exits per door, follows and unfollows per week, and
30-day retention of campaign follows (compare with the WJPC giveaway's 60 follows). The giveaway cap (≤3 a year,
event-tied) treats the whole Advent as **one** campaign, not 4 giveaways. Record that interpretation as an owner
decision.

## Gaps

- No public per-day curve for any **Instagram** advent or daily-quiz campaign: reach, replies or unfollows by day.
- No benchmark for **Story DM replies when a prize is offered**; the Rival IQ medians are for Stories that didn't ask
  for replies.
- Retention after giveaways rests on **one** small 2020 case of sponsored loop giveaways; everything else is
  vendor claims.
- The official Recommendation Guidelines text on contests couldn't be fetched (Help Center page empty, Transparency
  Center URL 404). The wording here comes from Buffer's Threads quote and the owner's earlier note.
- Mosseri's "too many giveaways" line is a reported paraphrase, not a verbatim quote.
- No evidence on puzzle, cubing or board-game brands' Instagram advent results; Ravensburger's web advent publishes
  no numbers.
- AoC stats include catch-up solves, and difficulty is mixed in with fatigue.
- Our own Story baseline (views, exits, replies per Story) wasn't available here. **Pull it from Insights before
  setting targets.**
- Legal: Czech/EU rules for free consumer contests weren't researched; check them before writing the rules.
