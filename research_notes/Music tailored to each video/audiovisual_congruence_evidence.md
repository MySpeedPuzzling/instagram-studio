# Matching music to visuals (audiovisual congruence): evidence and rules for tailored music in short social videos (Nov 2026)

Scope: which music–visual matches measurably matter (mood, tempo vs motion, pitch/brightness, timbre/texture, cuts on beats, structure vs narrative) when @myspeedpuzzling generates a music bed per video with ACE-Step 1.5 XL. This complements `research_notes/Local audio models and practice/short_video_audio_best_practices.md` and does not repeat it: tempo and persuasion (JCMC 2024), sound-on rates, sonic branding, loudness/mix, AI-music disclosure, and the unsourced "40% beat-sync completion" claim are all covered there.

Evidence labels (same as the sister note):
- **[Strong]** several peer-reviewed experiments or a review/meta-analysis with a consistent result
- **[Moderate]** one or two peer-reviewed experiments, or a large benchmark with stated method
- **[Weak]** a thesis, a secondary summary, or an abstract fragment only
- **[Opinion]** craft advice, no data
- (older) = before 2018. Most of the core congruence research is older; it is lab work with film clips or simple animations, not phone Reels.

Researched 18 Nov 2026 by web search. Several papers are known only from abstracts or citing papers; this is noted per item.

## 1. Film-music congruence theory: what the Congruence-Association Model (Cohen), Boltz, Iwamiya and Lipscomb found

### Takeaway
Two kinds of match matter and they work through different routes. **Semantic/affective match** (the music's mood matches the picture's meaning) steers how people *interpret* and *remember* a scene, and its effect is strongest when the visuals are ambiguous. **Formal/temporal match** (musical accents land on visual accents) makes the combination feel more "fitting" and effective, more strongly than matching the overall tempo to the speed of the motion. Accent sync matters most for simple visuals (like MSP's motion graphics) and less for complex live footage.

### Cited Findings
- **CAM structure [Strong as a framework, older roots].** Marshall & Cohen (1988) started the "congruence-associationist" model: music directly shifts how *active/potent* a scene feels, while its effect on *evaluation* (good/bad) depends on how well the audio and visual structures match. Later versions (CAM-WN, "working narrative") add a cross-modal pathway between visual and musical structure processing and treat sound effects as their own channel. A soundtrack's appropriateness = structural congruence + the associations the music brings. — [Cohen 2013, OUP chapter (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345887151_Congruence-Association_Model_of_music_and_multimedia_Origin_and_evolution); [Cohen 2015, Music and the Moving Image](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279241876_Congruence-Association_Model_and_Experiments_in_Film_Music_Toward_Interdisciplinary_Collaboration); [review: Meanings of music in film](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ism/article/download/21279/20544)
- **Congruent music increases absorption [Moderate, older].** Cohen & Siau (2008): viewers of an action scene with congruent music were slower to react to "X" marks in the screen corners than viewers without music, read as deeper absorption in the film (reported via review). — [Meanings of music in film (academia.edu)](https://www.academia.edu/70271863/Meanings_of_music_in_film_from_a_cognitive_perspective)
- **Mood of music biases interpretation and memory [Strong, older].** Boltz (2001, *Music Perception* 18:427–454): ambiguous film clips with positive music, negative music or none. Music biased interpretation *and* later memory in a mood-congruent way (positive music: a man following a woman read as a long-lost lover, and positive objects such as a bouquet remembered; negative music: malicious intent). Effects held a week later; soundtracks changed *which objects* were remembered. — [Haverford full text](https://scholarship.haverford.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?httpsredir=1&article=1019&context=psychology_facpubs); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Musical-Soundtracks-as-a-Schematic-Influence-on-the-Boltz/d3479f4c5e98bcd971387342c2bc2cf9c999759c)
- **Mood-matched music and film are stored together [Moderate, older].** Boltz (2004, *Memory & Cognition*): when music and film match in mood, they are encoded into one integrated memory, supporting the CAM. Boltz, Schulkind & Kantra (1991) is the earlier memory study. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03196892)
- **Ambiguity amplifies music's influence [Moderate].** The more ambiguous the image, the more the score shapes interpretation (Boltz 2001 as cited by Lipscomb & Tolchinsky). — [Lipscomb & Tolchinsky](http://www.lipscomb.umn.edu/docs/FilmMusic_LipscombTolchinsky_final_asPublished.pdf)
- **Formal vs semantic congruence (Iwamiya) [Moderate].** Iwamiya (2013, OUP chapter) separates *formal congruency* (matching temporal structure) from *semantic congruency* (similar affective impression). Iwamiya et al. (2002, cited in a review) found musical tempo and tonality changed the perceived speed and brightness of the visuals. — [Iwamiya chapter context, OUP intro PDF](https://global.oup.com/booksites/content/9780199608157/9780199608157_chapter11.pdf); [Systematic review (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332027403_On_music's_potential_to_convey_meaning_in_film_A_systematic_review_of_empirical_evidence)
- **Accent sync beats tempo–speed matching [Moderate; Japanese abstract only].** Sugano & Iwamiya: in rating experiments, synchronising auditory and visual accents had a *larger* effect on perceived fit than matching musical tempo to visual speed; the two effects did not interact. Sync also made clips feel more complex/powerful, tempo matching did not. Interpreted as separate mental processes. — [CiNii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001206567584128)
- **Matching *change patterns* [Moderate].** Kim & Iwamiya (2008): perceived congruence comes from (a) synchronised temporal structure and (b) matching patterns of change; rising loudness or rising pitch fits an *expanding/approaching* on-screen text (telop). Iwamiya also tested rotation vs pitch modulation. — [Formal congruency between telop patterns and sound effects](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249978365_Formal_Congruency_between_Telop_Patterns_and_Sound_Effects)
- **Accent alignment and effectiveness ratings [Moderate, older].** Lipscomb (2005): 56 audio-visual composites; aligned ("consonant") accent structures were rated highest for sync and effectiveness, misaligned lowest; sync and effectiveness ratings correlated r = .96. Effectiveness ratings were less extreme than sync ratings. A related ASA abstract proposed that alignment matters more for *simple* stimuli than for complex film. — [Lipscomb pre-print](http://www.lipscomb.umn.edu/docs/ArticleSelectedReportsPrePublicationWithFigures.pdf); [JASA abstract](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/105/2_Supplement/1274/550157/Cross-modal-integration-Synchronization-of)
- **Impression vs perceived sync can diverge [Weak, abstract only].** Yamada (2011, *Japanese Psychological Research*): a "discrepancy between impression formation and perception of temporal congruency" in audio-visual stimuli. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1468-5884.2011.00493.x)

### Inferences
- For MSP's motion graphics (simple, flat, few objects) the best-supported tailoring lever is **accent sync**: number landings, piece snaps, rank flips on musical accents. Exact tempo ≈ motion speed is a weaker lever.
- Mood match is what shapes *how the post is read* (a "confession" as funny vs sad, a record as triumphant). Short, text-led posts are partly ambiguous without sound, so the music's mood will colour interpretation for the ~75–80% who listen.
- Changes should move together: rising pitch/loudness with zoom-ins, growing bars, approaching elements; falling with exits.

### Gaps
- Almost all of this is lab work with film clips or abstract animations from 1988–2013; nothing found replicates it on vertical phone video.
- Full texts of Sugano & Iwamiya, Kim & Iwamiya and Iwamiya 2013 were not read (effect sizes unknown).

## 2. Crossmodal correspondences (Spence and others): pitch–brightness/size/elevation, timbre–texture/colour, sounds–tastes, music–colour

### Takeaway
People consistently match higher pitch to brighter, smaller, higher-up things, and louder sound to brighter light. These mappings are **relative** (higher than the previous note), not absolute. At the level of whole music, colour links run mostly **through emotion**: lively major-key music goes with vivid, light, yellowish colours; slow minor-key music with dark blues and greys. Timbre–colour matches exist and seem driven by meaning. These are robust in labs; there is no evidence they change engagement, but they give a principled default for "what sounds like this palette".

### Cited Findings
- **Core correspondences [Strong as a review, older].** Spence (2011, *Attention, Perception & Psychophysics*, ~1400 citations) reviews consistent matches between pitch, loudness, size, brightness and spatial position (e.g. loudness–brightness, pitch–elevation, pitch–size). Three proposed mechanisms: structural, statistical (learned from the world) and semantic. They affect how audio-visual events are grouped and attended (Spence & Parise 2012). — [Spence 2011 PDF](http://www.daysyn.com/Spence_-_2011_-_Crossmodal_correspondences_A_tutorial.pdf)
- **Pitch correspondences are relative [Moderate].** Spence (2019, *Multisensory Research*): pitch-based correspondences tend to be relative for each listener. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334205255_On_the_Relative_Nature_of_Pitch-Based_Crossmodal_Correspondences)
- **Pitch–size/brightness/height hold, pitch–hue does not [Moderate].** Spence & Di Stefano (2025, *Frontiers in Psychology*). The field has moved toward an *emotion-mediated* account rather than low-level mappings. — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1605110/pdf)
- **Music–colour via emotion [Strong].** Palmer et al. (2013, *PNAS*), US and Mexico: lively major-key music → vivid yellowish colours; somber minor-key music → dark blues/greys; colour emotion predicted from music emotion with ~95% accuracy (Palmer's quote). Whiteford et al. (2018, *i-Perception*) confirmed emotional mediation with stronger methods. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Music%E2%80%93color-associations-are-mediated-by-emotion-Palmer-Schloss/3a760f45753c3d037d8e95ef052f0749f5065eb8); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/05/130516151256.htm); [Whiteford 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/2041669518808535)
- **Timbre–colour [Moderate].** *Frontiers in Psychology* (2024): colour–instrument-timbre correspondences hold even with pitch register fixed (larger when register also varies); consistent with semantic mediation. Saitis & Wallmark (2024): timbral brightness modulates perceived pitch and possibly visual brightness. — [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1520131/full)
- **Timbre–visual design in marketing [Weak, abstract only].** Sunaga (2025, *Psychology & Marketing*): connotative congruence between instrumental timbre and visual design features affects consumer decisions. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.22222)
- **Sounds–tastes ("sweet/sour sounds") [Moderate, older].** Knöferle & Spence (2012): people reliably match tastes to sounds varying in pitch and timbre. (Common findings in this line: sweet ↔ higher, consonant, soft timbres; sour ↔ high, dissonant, sharp; bitter ↔ low, brassy; stated from the broader Spence literature, not verified in this pass.) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232231771_Crossmodal_correspondences_between_sounds_and_tastes)
- **Timing helps bind correspondences [Weak–Moderate].** A 2025 PMC review asks how temporal synchrony interacts with mid-level correspondences, citing Iwamiya's sync manipulations in film. — [PMC12996022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12996022/)

### Inferences
- Palette → mood → music is a sound default for the brief writer: bright, saturated MSP palettes call for major key, lively tempo, bright timbres; dark/night/Halloween palettes for lower register, minor/modal, darker timbres.
- Use *relative* pitch movement: rising motifs for things that grow, climb or get bigger (a record, a rank going up), falling for drops/endings. Absolute register matters less.
- Texture/material (wooden pieces, cardboard, glossy) can inform timbre choice (marimba/woodblock for wood, plucks for cardboard clicks), but this is craft extrapolated from the timbre–colour evidence, not directly tested.

### Gaps
- No study found linking timbre to *visual texture* (rough/smooth) in video specifically; only timbre–colour and timbre–design connotation.
- No evidence these correspondences change watch time, likes or shares.

## 3. Incongruent music, humour and "playing against" the picture

### Takeaway
Deliberate mismatch is well established as a film technique (counterpoint, irony), but the controlled evidence is thin and mixed. Incongruent music can *improve* recognition memory for a scene, and making a short video more congruent can make it *less* funny, but more incongruity is not reliably funnier and some mismatches simply confuse people and lower evaluations. Incongruity works when the viewer can "resolve" it (understands the joke).

### Cited Findings
- **Review [Moderate as a review].** Spence & Di Stefano (2026, *Frontiers in Psychology* 17:1728329), "Crossmodal counterpoint": few direct empirical tests of irony or humour; argues monitoring conflicting channels takes extra attention (no data cited for that). — [PMC12926423](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12926423/)
- **Incongruent music → better visual memory [Moderate, single study].** Damjanovic & Kawalec (2021): a comedic trailer with sad (incongruent) music gave better recognition memory for visual items than happy (congruent) music or no music; self-reported mood did not explain it. — via [Spence & Di Stefano 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12926423/)
- **Unresolvable mismatch hurts [Weak–Moderate, older].** Hung (2000): incongruent music–visual coffee ads worked when a familiar cultural frame made sense of them (e.g. action-film tropes), otherwise caused confusion and more negative product evaluation. — via [Spence & Di Stefano 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12926423/); see also [incongruity resolution chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-658-32201-4_13)
- **Music changes emotion read from faces (auditory Kuleshov) [Moderate].** Baranowski & Hecht (2017): happy/sad music shifted judgments of facial expressions toward the music's tone. — via [Spence & Di Stefano 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12926423/)
- **Short internet videos: congruence mutes humour [Weak, thesis].** Kent State thesis: rated humour of short (TikTok-style) videos; versions made more congruent were rated less funny. — [OhioLINK ETD](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=kent1627070680501086&disposition=inline)
- **More incongruity ≠ more humour [Moderate, music only].** Final-note study (*BMC Psychology* 2022): a single changed final note can be funny, but more tonally *congruent* unexpected endings (tonic, dominant) were rated funnier than very incongruent ones. — [PMC9526306](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9526306/)
- **Expected violations are funny [Moderate, not music].** Warren & McGraw (2016, *JPSP*): a violation raised humour, surprise did not. — [PDF](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/warren.mcgraw.2016.pdf)

### Inferences
- For humorous confessions ("I bought 40 puzzles this month"), the best-supported comic move is a *readable* mismatch: overly grand/epic or mock-serious music under a trivial admission, a sad trombone-style fall on the punchline. The genre must be instantly recognisable (cultural frame) so the joke resolves.
- Keep the musical mismatch "near": a clear stylistic exaggeration in a tonal key, not random dissonance.
- Don't use mismatch for data reveals or launches, where the goal is clear interpretation; congruence is safer there.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on incongruent music in short-form comedy with engagement outcomes; the thesis is the only short-video item and was read in fragments.

## 4. Musical fit in advertising: recall, liking, purchase intent

### Takeaway
Fit between music and the ad's message, product and audience reliably helps liking and memory, and more points of fit help more (Herget et al. 2022: about η² = .06–.12). But music by itself, especially fast music, can *reduce* recall of spoken content compared with no music, and earlier results are inconsistent because "fit" was defined loosely. Timbre fit helped both recall and liking.

### Cited Findings
- **Levels of fit [Moderate–Strong, one well-designed experiment].** Herget, Breves & Schramm (2022, *Musicae Scientiae* 26(1)): n = 178; six ads on four fit levels (how many of narration, product and target group match the music). Higher fit → better attitude to ad (η² = .12) and product (η² = .12), purchase intention (η² = .06) and memory (η² = .06); perceived fit differed strongly (η² = .33). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1029864920904095); [instrument paper 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320250682_Development_and_testing_of_an_instrument_to_determine_Musical_Fit_in_audio-visual_advertising)
- **Musical and voice fit [Moderate, older].** North, MacKenzie, Law & Hargreaves (2004, *J. Applied Social Psychology*): radio ads; musical fit improved recall of products, brands and specific claims, plus ad liking and purchase likelihood; explained as music priming related knowledge. (A "96% more likely to be recalled" figure circulates in a secondary source and could not be verified.) — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-effects-of-musical-and-voice-%E2%80%9Cfit%E2%80%9D-on-responses-North-MacKenzie/9d9c9b586c9f26db017223584f1a3b13213c47b6); [secondary figure](https://www.mrjournal.ro/docs/R2/05MR8.pdf)
- **Tempo and timbre congruity [Moderate, older].** Oakes & North (2006, *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 20:505–520): (1) slow-tempo music gave better ad content recall than fast-tempo; music overall reduced recall vs no music; (2) congruent timbre improved recall and affective response. Abstract only. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227875000_The_impact_of_background_musical_tempo_and_timbre_congruity_upon_ad_content_recall_and_affective_response)
- **Fit is multi-dimensional, results inconsistent [Moderate as review, older].** Oakes (2007, *J. Advertising Research*) narrative review: fit outcomes include purchase intent, brand attitude, visual and verbal recall, affect; different kinds of incongruity explain mixed recall results. Herget et al. (2018) argue the field could not reliably predict fit effects without a clear definition. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/250168716_Evaluating_Empirical_Research_into_Music_in_Advertising_A_Congruity_Perspective)
- **Attention moderates [Weak, title/abstract only].** "Attention required for advertisement pitch, tempo and timbre to influence the perception of product features." — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373704093_Attention_Required_for_Advertisement_Pitch_Tempo_and_Timbre_to_Influence_the_Perception_of_Product_Features)

### Inferences
- "Fit" for MSP = music matches (a) the message/story beat, (b) the subject (puzzling: calm focus, clicks, wood, satisfaction) and (c) the audience (adult hobbyists, competitive but cosy). Herget's results say each extra point of fit adds.
- Fast tempo may compete with reading dense text (Oakes & North on recall); the JCMC 2024 tempo result in the sister note found no memory cost. Combined: fast beds are fine for simple reveals, keep moderate/slow for text-heavy slides.

### Gaps
- No ad-fit study on vertical short video or organic posts; no study links fit to *sharing*.

## 5. Recent (2018–2026) short-form/online video work: datasets, benchmarks, AI soundtrack fit

### Takeaway
The ML field operationalises video–music fit along four dimensions: **rhythm/temporal sync, emotion, theme and cultural context**, plus tempo, key, instruments and genre for generation. Humans agree well on these judgements (~85% accuracy on HarmonySet's multiple-choice tests) while models still struggle, especially with rhythm. No dataset paper shows *which* dimension predicts engagement; peer-reviewed short-form engagement studies on music fit were not found.

### Cited Findings
- **HarmonySet (CVPR 2025) [Moderate].** 48,328 user-made videos with background music (458.8 h); labels for rhythm/sync, emotion, theme, culture; 58% of items have key timestamp annotations; three annotators each, 92% of a 10% sample rated high agreement. Multiple-choice accuracy: humans 85.3% rhythm, 84.5% emotion; Gemini-1.5 Pro 41.8% / 44.4%; culture weakest for all models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01725v1); [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Zhou_HarmonySet_A_Comprehensive_Dataset_for_Understanding_Video-Music_Semantic_Alignment_and_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- **Other datasets [Moderate].** MuseChat (CVPR 2024, 98,206 YouTube-8M items; Recall@1 32.8 vs 20.7 baseline); BGM909, SVM-10K, MMTrail (290k videos); TT-150K from TikTok (one track reused across many videos). — [MuseChat](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Dong_MuseChat_A_Conversational_Music_Recommendation_System_for_Videos_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); [Cross-modal VAE micro-video BGM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07268)
- **Retrieval models [Moderate, preprints].** A 2026 recommendation paper combines semantic retrieval with temporal reranking (VidMuse benchmark; reranked model 18.3 vs PE-AV 11.5), i.e. semantic match first, timing second. — [arXiv 2607.05971](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05971); [workshop PDF](https://mlforaudioworkshop.github.io/accepted_submissions_2026/CameraReadys%204-83/71/CameraReady/71_doh_arxiv.pdf)
- **Generation conditioned on fit attributes [Weak–Moderate, preprints].** VIBE (2026) fine-tunes background music generation on 60k pairs, using an LLM to extract tempo, key, instruments, genre, atmosphere per clip; evaluated on ReelBench. "Video Echoed in Music" (2025) aligns semantic, temporal and rhythmic features. — [VIBE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30125); [arXiv 2511.09585](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09585)
- **Engagement and stimulation [Moderate, preprint; from sister note].** Behavioural engagement peaks at moderate audio-visual stimulation (arXiv 2604.19995) — see sister note section 1.

### Inferences
- The brief writer can mirror the ML attribute set: per video, specify emotion, theme, cultural cue, tempo, key/mode, instruments, plus a list of sync points (timestamps). That is roughly what ACE-Step-style prompts can take.
- Model rhythm-matching is weak, so beat alignment should be done in the edit (place visual accents on the generated grid, or time-stretch), not trusted to the generator.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found linking measured music–video fit to Reels/TikTok watch time, shares or saves.
- No published human-rated fit study of *AI-generated* soundtracks vs human-picked music for short video (only automatic metrics in generation papers).

## 6. What to measure ourselves, and rules for the brief writer

### Takeaway
Measure a few cheap proxies per video (beat-to-accent offset, tempo vs cut rate, energy curve vs motion curve, mode/brightness vs palette) and track them against insights; the evidence says accent sync and mood match matter most, exact tempo match least. The rules below are inferences from sections 1–5, each tagged with its evidence strength.

### Cited Findings
- Accent sync > tempo–speed match for perceived fit (Sugano & Iwamiya) — [CiNii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001206567584128)
- Synchrony and effectiveness ratings move together (r = .96), stronger for simple stimuli (Lipscomb) — [Lipscomb](http://www.lipscomb.umn.edu/docs/ArticleSelectedReportsPrePublicationWithFigures.pdf)
- Music emotion predicts chosen colour emotion (Palmer 2013) — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Music%E2%80%93color-associations-are-mediated-by-emotion-Palmer-Schloss/3a760f45753c3d037d8e95ef052f0749f5065eb8)
- Rising pitch/loudness fits expanding/approaching on-screen text (Kim & Iwamiya) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249978365_Formal_Congruency_between_Telop_Patterns_and_Sound_Effects)

### Inferences

**Practical proxies to log per video (all computable locally; tools like librosa/essentia for audio, frame differencing for motion are standard but not evaluated here):**
- *Sync offset*: median |time from each planned visual accent (number land, snap, cut) to nearest beat/onset|. Target < ~50 ms; the exact perceptual threshold was not researched here (gap).
- *Tempo vs cut rate*: bpm / (cuts per minute); note whether cuts fall on beats, half-bars or bars.
- *Energy curve vs motion curve*: correlation between audio RMS/onset density per second and visual motion magnitude per second; also whether the climax of each is in the same second.
- *Valence/arousal vs palette*: music mode (major/minor) and spectral brightness vs palette lightness/saturation.
- Log these alongside the existing insights pull and compare against format medians after ~20 posts. Expect small, noisy effects at MSP's scale.

**Rules for tailoring music to a video (8–12):**
1. **Match the mood to the story beat first** [Strong for interpretation/memory; Boltz, CAM, Herget]. Write the brief from "what should the viewer feel at the end" (pride, amusement, anticipation, cosy). Music will colour how the post is read, most when text alone is ambiguous.
2. **Put musical accents on the visual accents** [Moderate; Iwamiya, Lipscomb]. Every number landing, piece snap, rank flip and cut gets a beat, hit or downbeat. The brief lists the timestamps; alignment is done in the edit, not left to the generator. This matters more than matching tempo to motion speed.
3. **Tempo: pick a grid that the cuts fit, not a "matched" speed** [Moderate]. Choose bpm so that key events land on beats or bar lines (e.g. events every 2 s → 120 bpm, one per bar). Don't chase fast tempo for "energy" on text-heavy slides; Oakes & North found fast music lowered content recall.
4. **Move with the motion** [Moderate; Kim & Iwamiya, Spence]. Rising pitch/loudness/riser for growing bars, count-ups, zoom-ins and climbs; falling figures for drops, exits and "oops" moments. Use relative movement; absolute register matters less.
5. **Palette sets mode and brightness** [Strong for association, untested for engagement; Palmer]. Bright, saturated, warm frames → major key, lively, bright timbres (bells, plucks, marimba). Dark/cool/night frames → minor or modal, lower register, softer or darker timbres.
6. **Timbre fits the subject** [Moderate; Oakes & North timbre congruity, timbre–colour work]. Prefer timbres that sound like the world on screen (wood/percussive clicks for puzzle pieces, warm acoustic for cosy, synth for tech/app launch). Avoid timbres that pull to an unrelated world unless it's the joke.
7. **Structure follows the narrative arc** [Moderate; CAM working narrative, salient-event findings in sister note]. Intro → build → reveal → button, with section changes at story changes. Musical phrase boundaries should coincide with slide/scene boundaries.
8. **For humour, mismatch on purpose and make it readable** [Weak–Moderate]. Mock-epic or mock-serious genre under a trivial confession, a falling "fail" figure on the punchline. The genre must be recognisable within a second, and stay tonal; random dissonance confuses.
9. **For data, launches and records, stay congruent** [Moderate]. Incongruity costs clarity and can lower evaluations when viewers can't resolve it (Hung 2000).
10. **Stack fit points** [Moderate; Herget 2022]. Check fit against message, subject (puzzling) and audience (adult hobbyists) separately; each additional match adds to liking and memory.
11. **Moderate, not maximal, intensity** [Moderate; sister note + Oakes & North]. Energy should peak once, at the reveal; don't run high energy, dense SFX and fast cuts throughout.
12. **Check fit with people, not only metrics** [Moderate; HarmonySet human vs model gap]. Humans judge fit reliably; models don't yet. Keep the owner's ear test (Works / Unsure / No-go) as the final gate, and log the proxies above to learn over time.

### Gaps
- No study ties any of these proxies to Instagram watch time, shares or saves; the rules are transfer from lab, advertising and film research.
- Perceptual tolerance for audio-visual accent offset in music video (how many ms early/late still feels "on the beat") was not researched in this pass.
- Texture–timbre and sweet/sour-sound correspondences have no evidence in video contexts; treat rule 6 as craft guided by research, not a tested effect.
